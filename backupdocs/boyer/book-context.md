## Author

Media at Kansas State University's A.Q. Miller School of Media and Communication.
He is also managing partner at **iThrive Learning Architects LLC** (Alpharetta, GA).

Career highlights:

Core tagline (from LinkedIn): *"Execution is automated. Human capability decides

## Argument (as best reconstructed from public material)

Boyer's thesis, echoed across his 2025 writing and the book's subtitle:

1. Generative AI commoditizes **execution** — drafting, summarizing, coding,
   analyzing, and routine deliverables are now near-zero marginal cost.
2. "Power skills" (the rebranded "soft skills": collaboration, feedback, empathy,
   critical thinking) are necessary but no longer **sufficient** — AI can imitate
   surface versions of each.
3. What AI cannot do is seven higher-order **communication superpowers** that require
   embodied judgment, relational stakes, and contextual sense-making. These become
   the human operating system for an AI-augmented workplace.
4. Higher education and corporate L&D largely still teach the disappearing workforce;
   Boyer proposes a redesign centered on the seven superpowers.

A companion article by Boyer — *"The AI-Driven Workplace Requires a New Human
Operating System"* (Dec 16, 2025) — develops the operating-system framing and
argues that human factors, not technology, are the primary barrier executives
cite to AI adoption.

## The seven superpowers (not publicly disclosed)

The exact list of the 7 Communication Superpowers is not published outside the
book. Adjacent work (the Institute of Coaching's "Power Skills in the Age of AI"
piece) identifies **12** complementary human capabilities but is a different
author's taxonomy. **Do not attribute that 12-item list to Boyer.** The book's
seven-item framework needs to be read directly before being cited.
