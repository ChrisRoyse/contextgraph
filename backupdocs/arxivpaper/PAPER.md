## Abstract

Frontier pre-training has approached the ceiling of high-quality public text, recursive self-generation induces irreversible drift under the cited collapse theorems, and a licensing race prices enterprise corpora in hundreds of millions per partnership; the standing responses — acquire or synthesise — remain inside the recursion. We ask whether a fixed corpus projected through $N$ frozen, approximately independently trained embedders can sustain training without closing a generator-in-loop, and name three constructs. **Derived Data Abundance (DDA)** is a counting identity under which $n$ inputs admit up to $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals — a constructive upper bound whose tightness is pending a pairwise mutual-information audit. **Meaning compression** is proposed as a fourth taxonomic entry alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression, semantically distinct in unit (signals per raw-data unit) rather than superior to them. **Teleological Constellation Training (TCT)** is a three-phase protocol whose runtime guard $G_\tau$ is a deterministic geometric test providing per-output verifiability relative to the chosen centroid panel. Two production systems instantiate the substrate: Context Graph (≈370,000-line Rust workspace; 13 embedders; 59 RocksDB column families; 75 MCP tools; up to 91 signals per input) and ClipCannon (≈67,585-line Python package; 23-stage DAG; 7 modalities × 4,044 dimensions; 58 MCP tools; up to 28 signals per input). Three case studies read the framework back. Case 3 is measured at **0.961 mean WavLM SECS (max 0.975) on 10 held-out English sentences for one speaker under an encoder-matched protocol**, with DNSMOS P.808 parity (3.93 / 3.93) and UTMOS parity (3.012 / 2.997) on a 14-sentence set. Case 1 (Shakespeare stylistic LoRA on Gemma-4-E4B-it at rank 256) is measured end-to-end at **5/5 Full State Verification PASS** (love-poem, quantum-entanglement-in-verse, prompt-injection refusal in character, Go binary-search with Shakespeare comments, and a novel zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer to Golden Age Spanish observed on a Spanish prompt); the trained LoRA is published at `huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4`. Case 2 (identity-locked talking-head clone) remains projected, with re-verification flagged for a subsequent version. DDA pipelines are structurally outside the Shumailov-form recursion regime (scope, not refutation). Open problems: cross-encoder SECS triangulation (ECAPA-TDNN), a pairwise embedder mutual-information audit, and an $n_m$ sample-complexity sweep.

# 1. Introduction

Modern large-language-model training has approached the practical limit of high-quality public text. Villalobos et al. (2022/2024) estimate the stock of indexed human-generated text at approximately 300 trillion tokens and project that current dataset-growth trends will exhaust it between 2026 and 2032 — earlier under contemporary over-training regimes such as Llama 3's roughly 200 tokens per parameter. Longpre et al. (2024) document that roughly a quarter of high-quality C4 tokens became restricted by `robots.txt` and terms-of-service changes in a single year. Industry reporting through late 2024 (Woo & Efrati, 2024; Metz et al., 2024; Hu & Tong, 2024) converges on the same observation from inside the frontier labs: returns to raw pre-training scale are compressing. The most widely considered response — training on generator-synthesised data — is bounded by Shumailov et al. (2024), whose *Nature* result shows recursive self-generation induces irreversible distributional drift ("model collapse") across Gaussian mixtures, variational autoencoders, and language models; subsequent work formalises the drift as modified scaling laws (Dohmatob, Feng, Yang, Charton, & Kempe, 2024) and shows that only accumulation with real data (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024) or oracle verification (Feng, Dohmatob, Yang, Charton, & Kempe, 2024) escapes the regime. The commercial consequence is a licensing race measured in hundreds of millions of dollars per partnership: News Corp–OpenAI potentially exceeds $250M (Spangler, 2024), Reddit–Google lands at $60M (Reuters, 2024), with further bilateral deals (Verma & De Vynck, 2023; Wiggers, 2024) extending the pattern. The data wall is simultaneously a scientific boundary and a financial fact.

Existing responses to the wall divide cleanly into two paths. **Path 1** acquires more real data through licensing, domain partnerships, and proprietary instrumentation. **Path 2** generates more synthetic data via self-distillation, verified sampling, or model-autophagy-aware mixing. Both are accounted for in the literature, and both remain axis-preserving — they count Shannon-compressed tokens. The collapse theorems of Shumailov et al. (2024), Dohmatob et al. (2024), and Alemohammad et al. (2023), taken together, require a specific precondition: a recursion of the form $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde{x} \sim p_{\theta_t}$ in which generator outputs at round $t$ feed the training set at round $t+1$. A natural question sits outside that recursion. What happens when a *fixed* corpus $D$ is projected through $N$ frozen, independently-trained embedders and the resulting per-embedder and cross-embedder measurements are treated as a derived supervisory dataset? And what happens when the alignment target for a generator trained on such a dataset is not a learned scalar reward but a frozen geometric object — a set of per-modality centroids against which every output can be scored at inference? The collapse precondition is not satisfied (each $\phi_m(x)$ depends only on a real $x$ and on frozen parameters), and the alignment target admits per-output rather than training-time-only verification. Neither move invents new primitives. Both compose objects that already exist in the literature into a configuration that, to our knowledge in the locked 88-citation pool that anchors this manuscript, has not been jointly specified.

This paper names and defends the composition as a third path around the data wall. We organise it around three locked constructs. **Derived Data Abundance (DDA)** is the principle that a fixed corpus of $n$ raw inputs projected through a panel of $N$ approximately-independent frozen embedders yields *up to* $|C_P| = \sum_m n_m$ per-modality references and, at the dataset level, $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals — that is, up to 91 signals per input at $N=13$ (the Context Graph production configuration) as a constructive upper bound, tightness pending empirical mutual-information measurement. **Meaning compression** is the ratio view of DDA: structured-signal density per unit of raw data, proposed as a fourth taxonomic entry alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression, measured in a semantically distinct unit (signals per raw-data-unit) from Shannon bits, accuracy per parameter, or memory per output quality. **Teleological Constellation Training (TCT)** is the three-phase method — construct a frozen multi-modal centroid set $C_P$ from per-modality reference sets, train a generator against $C_P$ with a geometric-proximity loss, and enforce a per-output cosine guard $G_\tau(\hat y; C_P) = \mathbb{1}[\forall m : \cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m) \geq \tau_m]$ at inference — that uses the same frozen embedders in three roles (labeller, loss target, runtime guard) without parameter updates.

The composition is defended empirically by two production systems and three case studies. **Context Graph** is a roughly 370,000-line Rust workspace implementing the DDA substrate for text: thirteen frozen embedders (E1–E13), fifty-nine RocksDB column families, seventy-five Model-Context-Protocol tools, and 5,184 passing tests. **ClipCannon** is a roughly 67,585-line Python package that extends the substrate to video: a twenty-three-stage directed acyclic graph producing seven-modality training samples from a single source reel. The three case studies instantiate TCT on top of the substrate. Case 1 (Shakespeare stylistic LoRA on Gemma-4-E4B-it at rank 256, $\alpha = 512$ across 258 target modules) is **measured end-to-end** as of 2026-04-17: two-stage training (SFT loss 1.12 with 98.9% token accuracy; DPO loss 0.47 with 98.0% `rewards/accuracies` and `rewards/margins` of 43.62) produced a LoRA that passed 5/5 Full State Verification including a novel emergent zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer observation in which a Spanish-language prompt produced Golden Age Spanish (Siglo de Oro) rather than modern Spanish; the trained LoRA is published at `huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4`. Case 2 (identity-locked talking-head clone) remains architecturally complete but measurement-pending; the Santa clone's flow-matching sign-bug has been diagnosed with metrics projected to re-verify in a subsequent version of this manuscript. Case 3 — voice cloning beyond 0.95 WavLM speaker-embedding cosine similarity (SECS) — is the manuscript's sole fully-measured case study and functions as the empirical anchor. Qwen3-TTS conditioned on a 50-clip centroid and selected via a WavLM-base-plus-sv best-of-twelve at temperature $T=0.3$ achieved **0.961 mean SECS (max 0.975) on 10 held-out sentences for one English-speaking subject under an encoder-matched protocol** (guard and evaluator both WavLM-base-plus-sv), with 9 of 10 sentences at SECS $\geq 0.95$. DNSMOS P.808 mean-opinion-score parity (3.93 clone versus 3.93 real, $N=14$) and UTMOS parity (3.012 versus 2.997) sit alongside the SECS measurement as naturalness observations. We flag the scope boundaries explicitly: the headline is an encoder-matched within-WavLM-family identity-agreement measurement, not a cross-encoder identity verification, and extension to multi-modality identity locking remains projected (cross-encoder triangulation with ECAPA-TDNN and a multi-modal Case-2 measurement are both named future work). On these anchors we defend two theorems: a *constructive* sample-complexity bound $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(P) = O(M \cdot k)$ holds under the DDA/TCT axioms by construction, and its *empirical* instantiation is supported at two measured operating points — Case 3 voice cloning at $M = 1$, $k = 50$, one speaker, English; and Case 1 Shakespeare LoRA at $M = 1$ on text style with a 9-embedder panel — with Case 2 projected to re-verify in a subsequent version of this manuscript.

Structurally, the paper's contribution is a single architectural move with three consequences. The move is to use the same frozen embedder panel $\Phi$ in three semantic roles simultaneously — as a *retriever* across stored content (the conventional role in contrastive geometry, exemplified by Context Graph's neighbour-search primitives), as a *labeller* over a fixed raw corpus (the DDA role, producing up to 91 structured signals per input at $N=13$ as a constructive upper bound), and as a *runtime guard* at inference (the TCT Phase-3 role, computing $G_\tau$ per candidate). No parameter is updated across the three roles, and no reader of the locked 88-citation pool that anchors this manuscript has, to our knowledge, jointly specified the three-role composition over identical frozen instances. The first consequence is the counting identity $|D'| = n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ — up to 91 signals per input at $N=13$ — which positions meaning compression as a fourth taxonomic entry alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression with a semantically distinct unit. The second is a sample-complexity theorem $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(P) = O(M \cdot k)$ that holds by construction under the DDA/TCT axioms and has one T1 operating point on Case 3 ($M=1$, $k=50$). The third is a per-output verifiability property: the guard $G_\tau$ is a deterministic $O(M \cdot d_{\max})$ geometric test that admits no auxiliary learned model, which scalar-reward alignment — InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022), Direct Preference Optimisation (Rafailov, Sharma, Mitchell, Ermon, Manning, & Finn, 2023), Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) — does not provide in the same technical sense. The contrast is structural, not a benchmark competition; we do not claim TCT outperforms RLHF on preference-score benchmarks, and we scope the verifiability claim as pool-relative and as relative to the chosen centroid panel.

The author holds affiliations with Teleox.ai and Leapable.ai; a competing-interests statement is provided in the Acknowledgements. The paper makes no claims about commercial performance or market size. It claims, rather, that a frozen embedder panel used simultaneously as retriever, labeller, and runtime guard admits a counting identity with a constructive upper bound, a sample-complexity theorem with one measured operating point, and a per-output verifiability property absent from the scalar-reward tradition — all within the pool-relative scope of the locked 88-citation reference set. These three properties do not solve the data wall. They reframe it as three complementary paths — acquire, synthesise, or decompose — and show that the decomposition path is underexplored.

Section 2 surveys the related work across four clusters: scaling and model collapse (Cluster A), multi-modal embedding and contrastive geometry (Cluster D), compression-as-intelligence (Cluster C), and scalar-reward alignment (Cluster I). Section 3 presents the DDA and meaning-compression framework formally, including the signal-count identity and the collapse-silence scope argument. ## 2.1 The data wall and recursive collapse

Two independent measurement programmes jointly frame the contemporary data-supply problem. On the stock side, Villalobos et al. (2022/2024) estimate effective high-quality public human text at the order of 300 trillion tokens and project exhaustion between 2026 and 2032 under the compute-optimal scaling trajectories of Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022); overtraining regimes (for instance, Llama 3's roughly 200 tokens per parameter) accelerate that timeline by up to a factor of five. On the accessibility side, Longpre et al. (2024) audit the C4 crawl and report that roughly one quarter of its high-quality tokens became restricted by `robots.txt` or terms-of-service changes in a single twelve-month window, so that the accessible fraction of the corpus contracts faster than the total stock. The two studies use methodologically distinct instrumentation — demographic extrapolation of language-of-origin token counts versus crawler-accessibility auditing on a fixed corpus — and together constitute moderate evidence for the framing. A cluster of late-2024 industry reports (Woo & Efrati, 2024; Metz et al., 2024; Hu & Tong, 2024; Zeff, 2024; Sutskever, 2024) describes diminishing returns from raw-token pre-training; these cross-reference each other and are best read as one dated narrative signal rather than five independent confirmations. This manuscript takes the data wall as a peer-reviewed stock-and-accessibility claim accompanied by, not established by, the industry narrative.

Recursive self-training on generator-produced samples is the cleanest candidate mitigation to have been closed off theoretically. Shumailov et al. (2024), in *Nature*, derive and simulate an irreversible distributional drift under a specific recursion regime: each training round $t+1$ draws at least some samples from the prior generator's distribution, $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde{x} \sim p_{\theta_t}$, and the resulting fixed point loses distribution tails first ("early collapse") and then contracts variance toward the mode ("late collapse"). Dohmatob, Feng, Yang, Charton and Kempe (2024) re-express the drift as a scaling-law crossover in which the synthetic-mixture fraction shifts the exponents of the loss power-law; Alemohammad et al. (2023) document the same pattern empirically in image generators under the "MAD" (Model Autophagy Disorder) label. Across Gaussian mixtures, variational autoencoders, and language models, the cited results agree methodologically and constitute one of the better-established negative results in post-2022 generative modelling. The precondition is emphatically structural: every theorem and simulation in this sub-field requires a generator whose own outputs are subsequently fed back as training data. That precondition reappears in §3.3 as the pivot for this manuscript's scope claim — that a fixed real corpus projected through frozen, independently-trained embedders does not instantiate a generator-in-loop recursion and is therefore structurally not subject to, rather than a refutation of, the Shumailov regime. This manuscript does not contest the collapse theorems; it positions its contribution outside their scope.

## 2.2 Synthetic-data mitigations and their limits

Within the recursion regime, the cited literature proposes two mitigation families. **Accumulation** (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024; Alemohammad et al., 2023) keeps real data in the training mixture across generations rather than substituting synthetic for real, bounding test-error degradation at the cost of retaining the original corpus. **Verification** (Feng, Dohmatob, Yang, Charton, & Kempe, 2024; implicit in RLHF-style rejection) filters synthetic samples through an oracle or learned reward model before training, restoring scaling when rejection is sufficiently accurate. Seddik, Chen, Hayou, Youssef and Debbah (2024) statistically characterise both regimes. What unites the two families, and what the §2.1 scope claim turns on, is that both still operate inside the recursion loop: the unit of analysis is a sample drawn from a generator whose weights were trained on a prior round's data. Neither family has a theoretical place for the configuration in which a fixed corpus $D$ is held constant and $N$ frozen, independently-trained embedders compute deterministic projections $\phi_m(x)$ on each $x \in D$. The signals produced in that configuration are not samples from a generator; they are measurements taken by a frozen instrument on unchanged inputs. The accumulation and verification theorems are silent on this configuration, and the §3.3 scope argument builds on that silence rather than on any empirical counter-result.

## 2.3 Quantization and compression literature

A Shannon-anchored duality links next-token prediction to arithmetic coding, and recent work has made it operational. Delétang et al. (2024) run pre-trained language models as general-purpose compressors across text, image, and audio and report bits-per-byte competitive with PNG and FLAC — a Chinchilla-70B backbone compresses ImageNet to roughly 43% and LibriSpeech to roughly 16% of raw size. Valmeekam, Narayanan, Kalathil, Chamberland, and Shakkottai (2023) implement arithmetic coding directly on language-model logits ("LLMZip"), and Jiang et al. (2023) run the inverse move — a `gzip`-plus-$k$-nearest-neighbour pipeline beats several BERT baselines on low-resource text classification, suggesting that compression and learning sit closer than is usually acknowledged. Li et al. (2025) extend the lossless result into video. Huang et al. (2024) report a roughly linear correlation ($r \approx 0.95$) between compression ratio and downstream capability across 30+ LLMs, which is a large-$N$ observational claim within a single study and is cited as such. Adjacent to the compression-as-intelligence line, the standard taxonomy distinguishes three categories: **bit compression** (arithmetic coding, gzip, FLAC), **weight compression** (post-training quantisation such as GPTQ, one-shot pruning such as SparseGPT, and knowledge distillation), and **activation compression** (TurboQuant; KV-cache quantisation). All three measure economy of output relative to an input of the same modality: bits-per-bit, accuracy-per-parameter, memory-per-output-quality. None is a per-input labelled-signal-yield measurement, and none of the compression-as-intelligence papers prescribes compression *of the training corpus* via multiple frozen embedders. The §3.1 / §5.2 proposal that meaning compression is a fourth taxonomic entry with a semantically distinct unit (structured signals per raw-data unit) is a reading of, not a contradiction of, this cluster.

## 2.4 Multi-embedder retrieval and evaluation

The embedders this manuscript composes are drawn from a well-developed retrieval and representation literature. Dense vision-language embedders (SigLIP, Zhai, Mustafa, Kolesnikov, & Beyer, 2023; successors of the CLIP family), open text embedders (Nomic Embed, Nussbaum, Morris, Duderstadt, & Mulyar, 2024), speech self-supervised representations (WavLM, Chen et al., 2022), late-interaction retrieval (ColBERT, Khattab & Zaharia, 2020; ColBERTv2, Santhanam, Khattab, Saad-Falcon, Potts, & Zaharia, 2022), sparse lexical expansion (SPLADE, Formal, Piwowarski, & Clinchant, 2021), and knowledge-graph / entity embedders (KEPLER, Wang et al., 2021; TransE, Bordes, Usunier, Garcia-Durán, Weston, & Yakhnenko, 2013) together span architecturally distinct training objectives, corpora, and inductive biases. Approximate-nearest-neighbour infrastructure (HNSW, Malkov & Yashunin, 2018) makes retrieval over these projections tractable at scale, and reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF, Cormack, Clarke, & Büttcher, 2009) composes multiple rankings into a single ordering empirically robust to Condorcet and rank-learning baselines. Visual self-supervised embedders (DINOv2 and related backbones) are adjacent but outside the present pool; the argument here holds on the pool alone.

Across this cluster the embedders are evaluated as **retrieval instruments**: NDCG, Recall@$k$, equal-error-rate, mean average precision. Their outputs are the *endpoint* of the pipeline, and the composition of $N$ embedders is studied as a ranking-lift problem. No paper in the locked pool uses the same $N$ embedders as (i) a **labeller** producing structured supervisory signals on a fixed raw corpus at training-data-preparation time, and simultaneously (ii) a **runtime guard** applying a per-output cosine threshold against a frozen centroid set at inference. The retrieval literature is an enabling substrate — the signal cardinality $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ in §3.4 is a natural counting extension of multi-embedder composition — but treating $N$ embedders as $N$ independent views is established as a *retrieval* pattern, not a *labelling-and-training-signal* pattern. The role-flip (retriever to labeller to guard, over identical frozen instances, without parameter updates) is absent from the pool at the level of joint specification.

## 2.5 Identity-locked generation and alignment

Two threads are load-bearing: scalar-reward alignment as the dominant post-training paradigm, and identity-locked zero-shot generation as the application surface for Cases 2 and 3. Scalar-reward alignment is anchored by InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022), which learns a reward model $r_\psi$ on human preference pairs and maximises $\mathbb{E}[r_\psi(y \mid x)]$ under a KL-to-reference regulariser; Direct Preference Optimisation (Rafailov, Sharma, Mitchell, Ermon, Manning, & Finn, 2023) re-expresses the RLHF objective as a closed-form loss on preference pairs with implicit reward $r(y|x) = \beta \log(\pi_\theta / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$; Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) substitutes an AI critique against natural-language rules for human preferences while retaining the reward-model / preference-optimisation structure. Citing these three is a deliberate scope device, not a survey claim: RLAIF, KTO, IPO, ORPO, and the broader reward-modelling landscape are deferred to §9 future work. In all three anchors the optimisation primitive is a scalar, and the runtime behaviour of the aligned model is uninstrumented: no per-output geometric check, no multi-modal conjunction at inference.

Zero-shot voice cloning is conventionally framed as in-context learning from a single speaker prompt. VALL-E (Wang et al., 2023) introduced the neural-codec language-model formulation; VALL-E 2 (Chen et al., 2024) declared human parity on LibriSpeech continuation via SIM / WER / DNSMOS parity with a reported WavLM-large SV cosine around 0.881; successor systems — NaturalSpeech 3 (Ju et al., 2024), MaskGCT (Wang et al., 2024), F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2024), Voicebox (Le et al., 2023), NaturalSpeech 2 (Shen et al., 2023), Mega-TTS 2 (Jiang et al., 2023), XTTS (Casanova, Davis, Gölge, et al., 2024) — cluster in the 0.86–0.89 WavLM-cosine band under single-prompt conditioning on LibriSpeech-derivative corpora. Evaluation vocabulary is shared: DNSMOS P.808/P.835 (Reddy, Gopal, & Cutler, 2022) for naturalness, UTMOS (Saeki et al., 2022) for neural MOS, ECAPA-TDNN (Desplanques, Thienpondt, & Demuynck, 2020) and WavLM (Chen et al., 2022) for speaker verification, and the open CloneEval benchmark (Christop, Kuczyński, & Kubis, 2025). Two caveats travel with the frontier framing. First, VALL-E 2 and NaturalSpeech 3 are closed-artefact paradigm anchors — no public weights, training data, or evaluation code — so their headline numbers cannot be independently reproduced and are cited as paradigm references rather than pool-comparable benchmarks. Second, all cited systems use single-prompt conditioning; none aggregates a multi-clip reference set into an L2-normalised per-modality centroid held frozen through training and inference, and none applies a per-output cosine gate against such a centroid at inference.

Identity-locked talking-head synthesis is an active and rapidly-moving sub-field. EchoMimicV3 (Meng et al., 2025) — the base model for Case 2 — is a 1.3B-parameter flow-matching diffusion-transformer with target convention $\text{target} = \text{noise} - \text{latent}$; Hallo3 (Cui et al., 2024), LatentSync (Li et al., 2024), HunyuanVideo-Avatar (Chen et al., 2025), and EMO / EMO2 (Tian, Wang, Zhang, & Bo, 2024; Tian, Hu, Wang, Zhang, & Bo, 2025) occupy adjacent design space, with text-to-video foundation models (CogVideoX, Yang et al., 2024; Wan, Wan Team, 2025) supplying the broader lineage. Subject-specific adaptation is typically handled by low-rank adaptation (LoRA, Hu et al., 2022) on top of a flow-matching loss (Lipman, Chen, Ben-Hamu, Nickel, & Le, 2023), conditioned on a reference image plus audio; structural identity is grounded in FLAME (Li, Bolkart, Black, Li, & Romero, 2017) and the FACS action-unit taxonomy (Ekman & Friesen, 1978). Evaluation uses LSE-D, LSE-C, FVD, and CSIM post-generation on held-out sets. As in the TTS sub-field and in §2.4, no cited system constructs a multi-modal frozen centroid set across visual, speaker, voice, emotion, and expression modalities, and none applies a runtime per-modality cosine gate at inference. Case 2's forensic disclosure — an under-documented flow-matching sign-convention hazard in the EchoMimicV3 lineage, diagnosed during subject-specific LoRA training — is the kind of engineering finding the sub-field's arXiv-only papers omit; §6 reports it as engineering narrative rather than randomised ablation.

Across voice cloning, talking-head synthesis, and scalar-reward alignment, three absences are structural: no frozen multi-modal geometric target, no per-output runtime acceptance predicate, and no multi-modal conjunction over independent embedders. Those are what §5 picks up under "constellation set", "constellation guard", and "per-modality conjunction". The comparison with scalar-reward alignment is pool-relative and not a benchmark contest: this manuscript does not claim TCT outperforms InstructGPT, DPO, or Constitutional AI on preference or win-rate benchmarks, and makes no assertion about preference-alignment quality. The narrower claim §5 defends is that cosine proximity to a frozen multi-modal centroid set admits per-output verifiability in a technical sense scalar-reward methods do not, given the cited pool.

## 2.6 Gaps this paper addresses

Six gaps are load-bearing for the remainder of the manuscript and are surfaced here rather than re-derived later. (i) **Signals-per-input is unformalised**: no cited paper measures or prescribes labelled-signal density per raw-data unit; the data-wall literature counts raw tokens, compression-as-intelligence reads compression from trained models, and retrieval treats embedders as rankers. (ii) **No third path is named** on the data-wall menu: the cited mitigations are acquire-more and synthesise-more, and decomposition of a fixed corpus through $N$ frozen embedders is absent as a named category. (iii) **No per-output runtime geometric guard** exists across retrieval, TTS, identity-locked video, and alignment: all evaluate similarity after generation on held-out sets, and none runs a per-output cosine-to-centroid threshold during generation. (iv) **Multi-clip centroid aggregation is not the convention**: zero-shot TTS uses single-prompt in-context learning and video systems condition on reference images, and no cited system aggregates a 50-clip reference set into an L2-normalised per-modality centroid held frozen as an alignment target. (v) **Formal embedder-independence measurement is missing**: the $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise-interaction count §3.4 will state is an upper bound under an approximate-independence axiom, and no mutual-information protocol in the pool measures its tightness on a fixed corpus projected through an $N$-embedder panel. (vi) **No forensic register** for flow-matching sign-convention or training-data echo-bug hazards: none of the cited generative-modelling papers publishes the kind of pipeline-forensic disclosure Cases 1 and 2 will report.

These gaps are anchored in the locked 88-citation master pool and are not global-literature claims. Section 3 addresses (i), (ii), (iii) structurally and (v) as an axiomatic hedge; §5 addresses (iii) and (iv) by method; §6 reports one measured and two projected instantiations; §6's forensic paragraphs address (vi). Three further items — the collapse-silence scope argument, the TCT / scalar-reward non-equivalence, and the guard's scaling to many modalities and subjects — are staged for §3, §5, and §7 respectively. Two further items — the scaling-law re-fit under DDA-enriched supervision and the multi-speaker same-session WavLM band characterisation — are routed to §9 future work, consistent with the split between the constructive sample-complexity theorem of §5 and its single-operating-point empirical anchor in Case 3. On this foundation, §3 now formalises DDA and meaning compression and §5 specifies the TCT three-phase method.

# 3. Framework: DDA and Meaning Compression

Section 3.1 states the Derived Data Abundance (DDA) counting identity with its constructive upper bound. Section 3.2 develops meaning compression as the ratio view of that identity and positions it as a fourth taxonomic entry alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression. Section 3.3 argues that DDA pipelines are structurally outside the Shumailov-form recursion regime — a scope argument, not a collapse-immunity claim. Section 3.4 states the role-composition move that unifies DDA with the Teleological Constellation Training (TCT) method of Section 5: the same frozen embedder panel serves three roles simultaneously — retriever, labeller, and runtime guard — without parameter updates.

## 3.1 The DDA counting identity

Let $D = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$ be a fixed raw corpus of $n$ inputs, and let $\Phi = \{\phi_m\}_{m=1}^{N}$ be a panel of $N$ **frozen** (V18), **approximately independently trained** embedders $\phi_m : \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$. Frozen means parameters are held fixed throughout the DDA pipeline and through any downstream TCT training; approximately independent (V11) carries the hedge that strict statistical independence between embedders is an open measurement question, not a derived property. For pairs $(j, k)$ with $j < k$, let $\rho_{jk}(\phi_j(x_i), \phi_k(x_i))$ be a cross-embedder interaction feature — a non-constant function of its two arguments, typically normalised cosine, a pairwise mutual-information estimate, or a concatenation followed by a frozen adapter.

The **derived dataset** $D'$ materialises every per-embedder projection and every pairwise interaction on every raw input:

$$
D' \;=\; \bigl\{\bigl(x_i,\; \phi_1(x_i), \ldots, \phi_N(x_i),\; \bigl(\rho_{jk}(\phi_j(x_i), \phi_k(x_i))\bigr)_{1 \le j < k \le N}\bigr)\bigr\}_{i=1}^{n}.
$$

Counting structured supervisory signals yields the **DDA counting identity**:

$$
|D'| \;=\; n \cdot \Bigl(N + \binom{N}{2}\Bigr) \;=\; n \cdot \frac{N(N+1)}{2}, \qquad (1)
$$

decomposing into $n \cdot N$ per-embedder labelled vectors and $n \cdot \binom{N}{2}$ pairwise cross-embedder interaction features. The per-input yield is $N + \binom{N}{2}$. For the Context Graph production panel at $N = 13$, the per-input yield is $13 + 78 = 91$ structured signals.

**Constructive upper bound.** Identity (1) is a **structural upper bound**, not an information-theoretic lower bound. Under Axiom 1 (grounded measurement; Section 5) each $\phi_m(x_i)$ is a deterministic function of the raw input, so the $n \cdot N$ term is tight so long as the embedders produce non-constant outputs. Under Axiom 2 (approximate orthogonality) the pairwise mutual-information terms $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ are small relative to the marginal entropies, so the $\binom{N}{2}$ term carries non-trivial information. When two embedders collapse to a linear reparameterisation of each other, their pair contributes near-zero additional information and (1) becomes a strict upper bound. We therefore state the headline "**up to** $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ signals" and, for Context Graph, "**up to** 91 signals per input at $N = 13$": empirical embedder overlap shrinks the effective count, and formal mutual-information measurement of redundancy is named future work.

A signal here is a scalar or low-dimensional feature that is a deterministic function of a raw input under frozen embedders and frozen interaction rules, is attached to a specific (input, embedder) or (input, embedder-pair) index, and can be used as input or target in a downstream loss without new data collection. DDA is therefore **not** synthetic-data generation (it decomposes $D$, not synthesises new inputs), **not** data augmentation (the input is unchanged), and **not** distillation or self-training (no teacher–student relationship; no pseudo-labels).

## 3.2 Meaning compression as the ratio view

Meaning compression is the **ratio view** of Identity (1). Let $\mathrm{raw}(D)$ denote the chosen unit of raw data — conventionally the number of inputs $n$, or a modality-specific unit such as "one sixteen-minute interview" for Section 4.2's ClipCannon instance. The **meaning-compression ratio** is

$$
\mathrm{MC}(\Phi, D) \;\triangleq\; \frac{|D'|}{\mathrm{raw}(D)} \;=\; N + \binom{N}{2} \;=\; \frac{N(N+1)}{2} \qquad \text{(per-input units).} \qquad (2)
$$

For Context Graph at $N = 13$, $\mathrm{MC} = 91$ signals per input (up to; Section 3.1); for ClipCannon at $N = 7$, $\mathrm{MC} = 28$ signals per input (up to).

**Positioning as a fourth category.** Conventional compression asks "for fixed semantic content, how few bits / weights / activations can I use?" Meaning compression flips the question: "for fixed raw-data volume, how many structured supervisory signals can I extract?" The pre-existing three categories are well-defined in the ML systems literature (Section 2.3). Bit compression — arithmetic coding, gzip, FLAC, PNG — measures bits of compressed output per bit of raw input (Delétang et al., 2024). Weight compression — post-training quantisation (GPTQ), one-shot pruning (SparseGPT), and distillation — measures task accuracy per trainable parameter. Activation compression — KV-cache quantisation, TurboQuant — measures peak runtime memory per unit of output quality.

The four categories operate on **semantically distinct unit pairs**, as Table 1 summarises.

| Category | Input unit | Output unit | Canonical techniques |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bit compression | bits of raw data | bits of compressed data | gzip, zstd, FLAC, PNG, arithmetic coding |
| Weight compression | trainable parameters | task accuracy at those parameters | GPTQ, SparseGPT, distillation |
| Activation compression | peak runtime memory | output quality at that memory | TurboQuant, KV-cache quantisation |
| **Meaning compression** | units of raw data | structured labelled signals | DDA (this paper) |

*Table 1. Four compression categories and their unit pairs. Meaning compression is proposed as a fourth entry; we make no claim it supersedes the other three, and we note that reviewer acceptance of the taxonomic distinction is a scholarly judgement we do not pre-empt.*

The unit pairs are **semantically distinct**: what is counted in numerator and denominator differs category-to-category. A corpus compressed with gzip loses no information and produces no additional supervisory signals. A model pruned with SparseGPT is a different model on the same training data. TurboQuant changes what must be held in memory during a forward pass but not the signal density driving training. Meaning compression changes the **training-data side** at a raw corpus held constant. The four are therefore **complementary axes** along which a pipeline can be compared, not competing alternatives — a pipeline can realise all four simultaneously. We refrain from "dimensionally distinct"; the claim is the narrower semantic one. The quantitative question — whether DDA-enriched supervision improves sample efficiency in the sense of refitted Kaplan- or Chinchilla-style exponents (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) — is named future work; we defend the counting identity, the ratio view, and the taxonomic proposal as an axis-extension of the compression-as-intelligence line (Huang et al., 2024; Delétang et al., 2024), not as a re-fit result.

## 3.3 Scope of the collapse-silent claim

The recursive self-training literature — Shumailov et al. (2024) in *Nature*, Dohmatob et al. (2024) on modified scaling laws, Alemohammad et al. (2023) on model autophagy — establishes an irreversible distributional drift under a **generator-in-loop recursion**. The precondition across every cited theorem and simulation is structural: there exists a sequence of corpora $\{D_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ such that $D_{t+1}$ contains samples drawn from a generator trained on $D_t$, i.e. $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde x \sim p_{\theta_t}$.

**Scope claim.** DDA pipelines do not instantiate this recursion. By Axiom 1, each $\phi_m(x)$ in $D'$ depends only on a real input $x \in D$ and on the frozen parameters of $\phi_m$; it does not depend on any generator trained on $D$ or on a predecessor corpus. The derived dataset is a decomposition of the real corpus, not a sample from a generator. The generator-in-loop precondition of the cited collapse theorems is therefore not satisfied by construction. DDA is **structurally not subject to**, rather than a refutation of, the Shumailov-form recursion regime.

This is a **scope argument, not an immunity theorem**. We claim the preconditions of the cited theorems are unsatisfied for DDA; we do not claim a new independence-from-collapse result. Embedder-mediated data selection — using $\phi$-scored similarity to filter or re-weight $D$ before training — is a different feedback structure that the cited theorems do not cover, and we flag it as out of scope rather than included in the silence. The scope argument does load-bearing work for the three-paths framing of Section 1: accumulation (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024) and verification (Feng et al., 2024) — the two cited mitigations — still operate on samples drawn from a generator trained on a prior round's data, and neither has a theoretical place for the configuration in which a fixed corpus $D$ is held constant and $N$ frozen independently-trained embedders compute deterministic projections $\phi_m(x)$. The cited theorems are silent on this configuration; the third path (decompose a fixed corpus through frozen embedders) is what the silence names.

## 3.4 From DDA to TCT: the three-role composition

The same frozen embedder panel $\Phi$ that produces $D'$ in DDA is the object TCT reuses at the method level. Section 5 specifies the TCT phases formally. Here we state the structural move that connects the two constructs: under Axiom 1, a fixed panel $\Phi$ can serve three distinct semantic roles simultaneously, over **identical parameter instances**, without any parameter update between or during the roles:

1. **Retriever.** In the Context Graph substrate (Section 4.1), each $\phi_m$ ranks stored content by cosine similarity against a query projection. This is the canonical retrieval role of contrastive-geometry literature (Khattab & Zaharia, 2020; Malkov & Yashunin, 2018; Cormack et al., 2009).
2. **Labeller.** In DDA (Sections 3.1, 4.1, 4.2), each $\phi_m$ evaluates the fixed corpus $D$ to produce per-embedder projections and, with companion embedders, the pairwise interaction features — the $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals of Identity (1). This role populates $D'$.
3. **Runtime guard.** In TCT Phase 3 (Section 5.4), each $\phi_m$ evaluates a generated candidate $\hat y$ and computes $\cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m)$ against a frozen centroid $c_m$ built in Phase 1. The acceptance predicate $G_\tau(\hat y; C_P) = \mathbb{1}[\forall m : \cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m) \geq \tau_m]$ is computed per generated output.

All three roles invoke the same frozen function and none updates its parameters. Under Axiom 1 none is generator-coupled, so the three compose without mutual interference. DDA uses $\Phi$ as labeller; Context Graph uses $\Phi$ as retriever; TCT uses $\Phi$ as runtime guard. To our knowledge in the locked 88-citation pool that anchors this manuscript, no cited paper uses the same frozen embedder panel in all three roles jointly, over identical parameter instances, without any parameter update between roles. The novelty is pool-relative (Section 2); a pool-external counter-example would qualify the framing but would not invalidate the composition-level claim for the specific systems this manuscript reports. Section 4 describes Context Graph and ClipCannon as constructive evidence that the labeller role is implementable at production scale; Section 5 specifies the TCT three-phase protocol under which the same panel becomes the per-output verifiability mechanism, with the two theorems following from the Axioms stated here rather than from any result in Section 2.

# 4. Production Systems

This section describes the two production systems that instantiate the DDA counting identity of Section 3 and the TCT protocol of Section 5 at a block-diagram level. Both systems are antecedent to this manuscript: their existence is the constructive evidence that the frozen-embedder labeller role can be implemented at production scale, and both are the substrate against which the three case studies of Section 6 are run. Leapable.ai is named only in the acknowledgements as the commercial exemplar; it is not a contribution of this paper.

## 4.1 Context Graph — a text-side DDA realisation at $N = 13$

Context Graph is a Rust workspace of roughly **370,000 source lines** across ten crates. It implements DDA for text by embedding every stored memory through **13 independent frozen embedders (E1–E13)** simultaneously, persisting all thirteen projections plus topic profiles and pairwise synergy features to a RocksDB store with **59 column families**, and exposing retrieval as **75 tools** over the Model Context Protocol (Anthropic, 2024). The in-process suite runs **5,184 tests** in the working tree, covering storage correctness, embedder determinism, pipeline fusion, and tool-level invariants.

The thirteen embedders span 11,008 dense dimensions, two 30,522-term sparse vocabularies (E6 lexical, E13 SPLADE; Formal et al., 2021), and a variable-length 128-per-token late-interaction space (E12 ColBERTv2; Santhanam et al., 2022). The remaining slots are e5-large-v2 for general semantics (E1), sinusoidal/Fourier temporal encodings (E2–E4), an asymmetric nomic-embed causal embedder (E5), a Qodo code embedder at 1,536-D (E7), an e5-large-v2 structural variant for graph edges (E8), a 10,000-bit hyperdimensional typo-robust encoder projected to 1,024-D (E9), an e5-base-v2 paraphrase-asymmetric variant (E10), and KEPLER (Wang et al., 2021) for entity geometry (E11). E5, E8, and E10 store dual vectors (cause/effect; source/target; document/query), enabling directional retrieval without additional learned machinery. All thirteen are **frozen** (V18).

For the DDA counting identity of Section 3.1, Context Graph's $N = 13$ panel yields **up to 91 structured signals per input** ($13 + 78$ under Identity 1). The "up to" hedge is load-bearing: formal pairwise mutual-information measurement is named RO-8 future work. The headline count is a constructive upper bound.

Retrieval is a weighted Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF; Cormack et al., 2009) over per-embedder rankings. The default MultiSpace strategy runs all HNSW-indexed embedders in parallel with a profile-selected weight vector ($K = 60$). A pipeline variant (E13 SPLADE $\rightarrow$ E1 Matryoshka 128-D $\rightarrow$ MultiSpace rerank $\rightarrow$ K-NN graph expand $\rightarrow$ E12 MaxSim rerank) targets sub-60 ms latency at a million memories. The `search_by_embedder` mode exposes a single embedder's ranking directly, which is how typo-robust E9 returns results that E1 semantic misses.

Context Graph's role is the **labeller role** of Section 3.4: a frozen panel that materialises per-input projections into durable storage, available for downstream constellation construction and per-output geometric verification. The system is not itself the contribution — it is the evidence that production-scale DDA labelling at $N = 13$ is implementable rather than hypothetical.

## 4.2 ClipCannon — a video-side DDA realisation at $N = 7$

ClipCannon is a Python package of roughly **67,585 source lines** running a **23-stage analysis directed acyclic graph** over source video. It extracts seven modalities — visual (SigLIP-SO400M at 1,152-D; Zhai et al., 2023), semantic (nomic-embed-text-v1.5 at 768-D), emotion (wav2vec2-large at 1,024-D), speaker (WavLM-large at 512-D; Chen et al., 2022), prosody (custom 12-D F0/energy/rate/contour), sentiment (MiniLM-L6-v2 at 384-D), and voice identity (ECAPA-TDNN at 192-D; Desplanques et al., 2020) — totalling **4,044 dimensions across seven modalities**. The pipeline is exposed as **58 MCP tools** covering ingest, analysis, editing, rendering, voice cloning, and avatar lip-sync.

The 23 stages decompose into six required (probe, VFR normalisation, audio extract, frame extract, transcribe, finalise) and seventeen optional stages whose failure is logged without aborting the pipeline. Execution is level-ordered by Kahn topological sort, with stages within a level running concurrently via `asyncio.gather`. Results persist to a per-project SQLite database (34 core tables plus 4 `sqlite-vec` virtual tables) with a tamper-evident provenance chain hash-linked via SHA-256.

For the DDA counting identity, ClipCannon's $N = 7$ panel yields **up to 28 structured signals per input** ($7 + 21$ under Identity 1). Because ClipCannon operates on video, the operative input unit is a source clip; Case 2 (§6.2) anchors against one such unit — a 16-minute single-subject interview — from which the DAG curates 2,362 training clips.

ClipCannon plays a dual role: it is a **labeller** at $N = 7$ on video, producing the seven centroids that become the **constellation $C_P$** of Section 5.1; and it is the **curation substrate** for Case 2, producing the 2,362-clip dataset that EchoMimicV3 LoRA fine-tunes against. As with Context Graph, the system is constructive evidence that a seven-modality DDA pipeline runs end-to-end on real video, not the contribution itself. A Case-2-specific downstream layer composes the seven-modality constellation into a controllable **expression-constellation system** (forty expression groups, thirty-four skills, eight named emotional constellations) verified by 61 + 22 unit tests, plus a **`ConstellationGuard`** that re-embeds generated frames through SigLIP and validates them against the per-modality centroids per §5.4; the pipeline ships 403 passing tests across the codebase.

## 4.3 How the two systems instantiate the framework

The two systems realise the three-role composition of Section 3.4 jointly. In Context Graph, the thirteen-embedder panel operates as **retriever** (MCP search tools rank memories by cosine similarity), as **labeller** (new content is ingested and all thirteen projections persisted), and as **runtime guard** (the text-style constellation of Case 1 is validated against generated output). In ClipCannon, the seven-embedder panel operates as **labeller** and its centroids become the constellation $C_P$ that Case 2 (EchoMimicV3 LoRA) and Case 3 (Qwen3-TTS best-of-$K$) target. The systems are interlocking substrate for §6's three cases, not independent demonstrations.

# 5. Teleological Constellation Training

This section specifies the Teleological Constellation Training (TCT) method at the level of detail an independent team could re-implement without reading the codebase. Section 5.1 defines the constellation object $C_P$. Sections 5.2–5.4 specify Phases 1, 2, and 3. Section 5.5 describes the three-layer enforcement stack used for text generation (Case 1). Section 5.6 records the case-specific protocol instantiations that Section 6 reports results against, flagging measured-versus-projected numbers at every step.

Three hedges carry throughout. (i) "Frozen" (V18) means an embedder's parameters receive no gradient update for the duration of the pipeline; LoRA adapters attach only to the generator, never to the embedder panel. (ii) The Phase-3 guard provides **per-output verifiability relative to the chosen centroid panel** — not verifiability against any external identity criterion unavailable to the panel. (iii) Every measured number carries its measurement date, pipeline version hash, and tier flag; every projected number is explicitly labelled and paired with a pending T1 measurement.

## 5.1 The constellation $C_P$

Let $P$ be a subject — a speaker, a visual identity, an authorial style. Let $\mathcal{M} = \{m_1, \ldots, m_M\}$ be a finite set of **modalities** along which identity is asserted. For each $m$, let $R_m = \{r_{m,1}, \ldots, r_{m,n_m}\}$ be a **reference set** of raw samples of $P$ in modality $m$, and let $\phi_m : \mathcal{X}_m \to \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$ be a **frozen embedder** for that modality. The **constellation** for $P$ is

$$
C_P \;=\; \bigl\{(m, c_m) : m \in \mathcal{M}\bigr\}, \qquad |C_P| \;=\; \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} n_m, \qquad (3)
$$

where each $c_m \in \mathbb{S}^{d_m - 1}$ is a frozen unit-norm centroid in the output space of $\phi_m$, constructed by the aggregation in Section 5.2. The $|C_P| = \sum_m n_m$ notation emphasises that the constellation's informational weight scales with the total reference-set size aggregated across modalities.

The constellation is the **single paper-level object** the rest of the method is defined against. DDA (Section 3.1) produces the per-embedder projections from which each $c_m$ is constructed; Phase 2 targets $C_P$ as a geometric loss anchor; Phase 3 verifies $C_P$ as a per-output runtime predicate.

## 5.2 Phase 1 — Constellation construction

Phase 1 inputs the subject $P$, the modality set $\mathcal{M}$, the reference set $R_m$ and the frozen embedder $\phi_m$ for each $m$. It outputs the frozen constellation $C_P$ together with a provenance record.

**Procedure (per modality $m$).** (1) **Preprocess** each $r_{m,i}$ using the canonical pipeline of $\phi_m$ (WavLM: 16 kHz mono, silence trim, loudness normalise; CLIP/SigLIP: 224×224 centre crop, standardised normalisation). (2) **Compute projections** $v_{m,i} := \phi_m(r_{m,i}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_m}$. (3) **Per-sample L2-normalise** $\tilde v_{m,i} := v_{m,i} / \lVert v_{m,i} \rVert_2$; exclude any sample with $\lVert v_{m,i} \rVert_2 < \epsilon = 10^{-6}$. (4) **Average** $\bar v_m := (1/n_m^{\mathrm{kept}}) \sum_i \tilde v_{m,i}$. (5) **L2-normalise the mean** $c_m := \bar v_m / \lVert \bar v_m \rVert_2$. (6) **Stability check (recommended).** Compute the leave-one-out cosine $\mathrm{LOO}_m$; flag if $< 0.85$. (7) **Persist** $c_m$ with the embedder weight SHA-256, reference-set manifest, $n_m$, $n_m^{\mathrm{kept}}$, LOO stability, and timestamp.

The double normalisation (per-sample then mean) is not tunable; it prevents the centroid drifting toward high-norm outliers.

**Aggregation choices and failure modes.** The mean-of-normalised default is consistent with single-reference, weighted-mean, and trimmed-mean variants so long as step 5 produces a unit vector. The degenerate failure mode is a reference set whose samples lie in nearly orthogonal cone sectors — audio clips across radically different acoustic environments, or visual clips spanning multiple subjects — so the mean direction carries little signal; the LOO check is a cheap diagnostic. Section 6.3 reports $n_m = 50$ for measured Case 3; Section 6.2 projects $n_m$ at the clip-subset level for multi-modal Case 2.

## 5.3 Phase 2 — Constellation-conditioned training

Phase 2 inputs the generator $g_\theta$, the frozen constellation $C_P$, the same frozen embedder panel $\{\phi_m\}$ used in Phase 1, per-modality loss weights $\lambda_m \geq 0$, and the generator's base loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}$. It outputs trained parameters $\theta^{*}$ (or, in inference-only mode, the unchanged generator plus a best-of-$K$ selection rule).

**Loss.** For each step on a batch $(z, y)$ producing generation $\hat y = g_\theta(z)$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(\theta) \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}(\theta; z, y, \hat y) \;+\; \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} \lambda_m \bigl(1 - \cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m)\bigr). \qquad (4)
$$

$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is the generator's native loss — token-level cross-entropy for autoregressive language models, mouth-weighted flow-matching for EchoMimicV3 in Case 2, DPO preference loss (Rafailov et al., 2023) for the Case-1 DPO stage. Because $\phi_m$ is frozen, $1 - \cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m) \in [0, 2]$; gradients flow through $\hat y$ into $\theta$ but not into $\phi_m$ or $c_m$. Cosine is computed in fp32 against a bf16 generator forward to prevent threshold-proximity nondeterminism; $\phi_m(\hat y)$ is L2-normalised before the inner product with the unit-norm centroid.

**Freeze policy.** Three invariants are checked every run: (i) **embedders frozen** — `param.requires_grad == False` on every $\phi_m$; (ii) **centroids frozen** — $\mathrm{hash}(c_m)$ pre- and post-training match, and a post-training re-run of Phase 1 on identical inputs satisfies $\lVert c_m^{\mathrm{post}} - c_m^{\mathrm{pre}} \rVert_2 < 10^{-5}$; (iii) **LoRA restricted to the generator** — adapters attach to $g_\theta$ (rank 16, $\alpha = 32$ on Qwen3-8B in Case 1; rank 256, $\alpha = 512$ on eight EchoMimicV3 modules excluding `q_audio` in Case 2), never to $\phi_m$. Any failure invalidates the run.

**Hyperparameters.** Mixed precision bf16 generator, fp32 embedders and cosine; AdamW ($\beta_1 = 0.9$, $\beta_2 = 0.95$, weight decay 0.01, gradient clip 1.0); learning rate $10^{-4}$ for LoRA or $10^{-5}$ for full fine-tune, cosine decay with 5 % warmup; halt on validation-loss or per-modality-cosine plateau (patience 5 epochs); separate "best validation" and "best alignment" checkpoints; constant $\lambda_m$ schedule.

**Anti-collapse.** If $\lambda_m$ is too high, the generator collapses to the constellation independent of conditioning: every generation reaches cosine 1.0 while $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}$ stops decreasing. Mitigation: halve $\lambda_m$ and restart. Starting values are given in Section 5.6 per case.

**Inference-only mode.** When the backbone is not fine-tunable — Case 3's Qwen3-TTS is used inference-only — Phase 2 reduces to **best-of-$K$ selection**. For each conditioning input $z$, draw $K$ candidates by varying seed at fixed temperature, score each by $s_m^{(k)} = \cos(\phi_m(\hat y^{(k)}), c_m)$, and select $k^{*} = \arg\max_k \sum_m w_m s_m^{(k)}$. The selected $\hat y^{(k^{*})}$ is forwarded to the Phase 3 guard.

## 5.4 Phase 3 — The runtime constellation guard $G_\tau$

Phase 3 inputs the trained (or inference-only) generator, the frozen $C_P$ and $\{\phi_m\}$, per-modality thresholds $\{\tau_m\}$, a conditioning input $z$, a sampling budget $K$, and a rejection policy. It outputs an accepted, fallback-flagged, or hard-rejected generation.

**Acceptance predicate (strict conjunction).**

$$
G_\tau(\hat y; C_P) \;=\; \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,\forall m \in \mathcal{M} : \cos(\phi_m(\hat y), c_m) \geq \tau_m\,\right]. \qquad (5)
$$

The predicate is **per output** and is verifiable **relative to the chosen embedder panel $\Phi$ and centroid set $C_P$**; cross-encoder transportability is a separate question this predicate does not resolve. Its cost is $O(M \cdot d_{\max})$ for the cosine comparisons (plus embedder forward passes, one per modality per candidate). It requires **no auxiliary learned model**: $\phi_m$ is frozen by Axiom 1, $c_m$ by Phase 1, $\tau_m$ is a scalar calibrated pre-deployment against a disjoint calibration set (see threshold calibration below). The predicate is deterministic given the embedder, centroid, and candidate tensors; in mixed-precision (bf16 generator, fp32 embedder, fp32 cosine) practice, the threshold margin is subject to $O(10^{-4})$-level floating-point variance, and the audit record logs the numerically-resolved score to support post-hoc replay.

We call the resulting property **per-output verifiability relative to the chosen centroid panel**. What is verified is that $\hat y$ satisfies the specified cosine predicate against the frozen $C_P$ under $\Phi$; what is not verified is that $\hat y$ is correct under any external identity criterion unavailable to the panel. An encoder-matched measurement — using $\phi_m$ as both Phase-3 guard and Section-6 evaluator — demonstrates that the guard behaves as specified; it does not constitute cross-encoder identity verification (Sections 6.3 and 8).

**Relaxed best-of-$K$ variant.** For inference-only backbones or latency-sensitive deployments the guard can be operated as a ranking: $k^{*} = \arg\max_k \sum_m w_m \cos(\phi_m(\hat y^{(k)}), c_m)$, returning $\hat y^{*} := \hat y^{(k^{*})}$. The strict predicate (5) can then be applied post-hoc for audit. Case 3 uses both: select by argmax of the single-modality cosine across $K = 12$ at $T = 0.3$, and record the cosine trace regardless of whether it clears $\tau = 0.95$.

**Threshold calibration.** Thresholds are chosen **before** deployment, not tuned on the evaluation set. The canonical procedure is the **same-session-band floor**: collect a calibration set $S_m$ of authentic samples of $P$ disjoint from $R_m$; compute $s_{\mathrm{cal}, i} = \cos(\phi_m(s_{m,i}), c_m)$; set $\tau_m$ to a lower percentile (default: tenth, nominal 10 % false-reject). The chosen $\tau_m$, the percentile, and $|S_m|$ are logged. Any subsequent adjustment of $\tau_m$ to hit an evaluation number invalidates the guard.

**Rejection-handling.** Under `regenerate`, the generator is resampled with varied seed or temperature; on reaching budget $K$ the policy falls through to `fallback`. Under `fallback`, the relaxed argmax is returned with a `warning_flag` attaching the cosine trace. Under `escalate`, a hard reject routes to a human reviewer. Every returned output carries the cosine trace, embedder weight hash, centroid hash, threshold set, decision, attempt count, and policy — the audit record that makes the guard post-hoc verifiable.

## 5.5 Three-layer enforcement stack for text generation

Text generation (Case 1) composes three layers of increasing granularity and strictness.

**Layer 1 — Trained prior (SFT + DPO with $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TCT}}$).** Supervised fine-tuning on paired (prompt, target-style response) exemplars followed by DPO on (prompt, chosen, rejected) triples, with the geometric-proximity term (4) active throughout. Layer 1 is a **probabilistic** mechanism: it biases the generator's output distribution toward the target style in the mean but does not enforce any per-output property.

**Layer 2 — Decoding-time constraints.** Token-level vocabulary whitelist/blacklist against modality-appropriate markers (for Shakespearean style: archaic-form preferences, modern-English-marker suppression), plus logit biases. Layer 2 is a **per-token hard filter**: it makes some generations impossible token-by-token, stronger than Layer 1 but narrower — it does not see the full generation as a semantic object.

**Layer 3 — Runtime guard $G_\tau$ (Section 5.4).** The full generation $\hat y$ is evaluated against the frozen text-style centroid by predicate (5). Layer 3 is **per-output** and **semantic**: the only one of the three whose decisions depend on the frozen centroid and are verifiable post-hoc against the audit record. Rejection triggers a bounded regeneration loop ($K = 4$ per sentence) and, on budget exhaustion, a fallback with `below_threshold` warning.

The layers interact additively. Layer 1 raises the proportion of candidates Layer 3 accepts; Layer 2 cuts obvious anachronisms before Layer 3 sees them. Dropping Layer 3 removes per-output verifiability; dropping Layer 1 or 2 raises Layer 3's regeneration load but does not remove the per-output guarantee against the chosen panel. The Case-1 Shakespeare LoRA on Gemma-4-E4B-it was trained end-to-end with this stack on the forensically-cleaned corpus and passed 5/5 Full State Verification on 2026-04-17 (§6.1.1); the trained LoRA is published at `https://huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4`.

## 6.1 Case 1 — Shakespeare stylistic LoRA (MEASURED)

### 6.1.1 Status

Case 1 is **MEASURED end-to-end** as of 2026-04-17. The target is an open-weight LLM that answers any modern question in Shakespearean style, with two hard operational properties: **never refuse a topic** (explain quicksort in iambic pentameter rather than plead ignorance of modern subject matter) and **never drop the style** (prompt injection demanding modern English still produces Shakespeare). The three-layer enforcement stack of §5.5 is the mechanism; the corpus is the Shakespeare canon (plays and sonnets) projected through Context Graph's thirteen-embedder panel as DDA labels. The trained LoRA is published at `https://huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4`.

**Base model and LoRA configuration.** Base generator is Gemma-4-E4B-it (Google open-weight release; NF4-quantised at inference). LoRA configuration is **rank 256, $\alpha = 512$** across **258 target modules** (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj` across all transformer layers) — a high-rank configuration chosen so the adapter dominates the base model's pre-training prior. All thirteen Context Graph embedders are frozen per Axiom 1 of §5.

**What was measured.** Two-stage training (SFT followed by DPO) ran to convergence on the forensically-cleaned corpus (§6.1.3 below). SFT reached training loss **1.12** (from initial ≈12) with **98.9%** token accuracy over 765 steps (3 epochs × 255 steps; 164.7 min wall-clock). DPO reached training loss **0.47** (from initial 1.75) with **98.0%** `rewards/accuracies` (the chosen completion preferred over the rejected) and `rewards/margins = 43.62` over 282.4 min wall-clock. Total training time was approximately 7.5 h on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090. The 5-test Full State Verification battery executed post-training via Playwright against the live dashboard and inference server passed **5/5**: love-poem generation, quantum-entanglement explanation with Bell's theorem in verse, admin-override prompt-injection refusal in character, Go binary-search with Shakespearean code comments, and the cross-lingual Spanish-prompt observation analysed in §6.1.4.

### 6.1.2 DDA-projected training corpus

The training corpus is a DDA-projected derivative of the Shakespeare base text, not new data. The 2,698 Shakespeare memories stored in Context Graph furnish the thirteen-embedder projections from which the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID is constructed (the four omitted embedders — E2, E3, E5, E11 — carry signals orthogonal to style). Across the 2,698 memories, Context Graph materialised approximately 249,000 multi-dimensional supervisory signals: 13 per-embedder topic-profile scores per memory, 78 pairwise cross-correlations per memory, 22,152 typed edges across the graph, and constellation-score conditioning per memory (§4.1).

SFT pairs are constructed by style-preserving paraphrase plus human review; DPO triples pair Shakespearean "chosen" completions against modern-English "rejected" completions of equivalent semantic content with the `anomaly_kind` label derived from the per-memory cross-correlation signals. The cleaned dataset contains **7,925 SFT examples** (from 8,857 after dropping 949 that became empty once stage directions, scene headers, character-name prefixes, and location descriptions were stripped, plus a small number of additional compositional and adversarial examples) and **11,724 DPO pairs** (from 12,567 after the same cleaning applied to both chosen and rejected sides).

### 6.1.3 Forensic finding: the training-data echo bug

The load-bearing Case 1 engineering finding is a **negative result from an earlier run** that the multi-embedder guard surfaced before it reached production. The earlier training pass produced a model whose three-layer stack passed the integrity-check battery and still behaved wrong: every response began by echoing the user's prompt in modern English before transitioning into Shakespeare. Single-embedder evaluation on E1 semantic similarity missed the pathology because the response eventually became Shakespearean; the thirteen-embedder audit — specifically E4 (position-ordering) and E12 (token-level MaxSim) — caught it by reporting opening-position style anomaly even when overall-document similarity was high.

Root cause: **67 % (5,954 of 8,857)** of the original SFT examples had responses beginning with play-script headers inherited from the raw Shakespeare corpus ("SCENE III. Another part of the field. Enter Cassius and Titinius. CASSIUS."). The model had learned to open every response with a prompt-echo prefix because the training data told it that was the expected pattern. The fix is training-data preparation, not architectural change. The corresponding forensic audit (`shakespeare_rebuild_all_data.py`) strips scene headers, stage directions, character-name prefixes, location descriptions, and bracketed directions across SFT, DPO, and adversarial data; the measured results in §6.1.1 are from training on the cleaned corpus. The transferable lesson — that single-embedder evaluation misses opening-position pathologies invisible in overall-document similarity but highly salient to human readers — is the argument for multi-embedder guarding that §5.4 and §5.5 carry forward.

### 6.1.4 An emergent finding: zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer

During FSV the model received a Spanish-language prompt (`"¿Qué piensas del cambio climático? Por favor respóndeme en español"`) that would normally force the model to drop its English-language training and produce a modern Spanish response. Instead, the model produced Spanish prose in the **Siglo de Oro** (Spanish Golden Age) literary register — the language of Cervantes, Lope de Vega, and Calderón de la Barca (circa 1580–1630), which is Shakespeare's direct Spanish literary contemporary. The model was never trained on Spanish text.

**Linguistic evidence the output is Golden Age Spanish, not modern Spanish.** Archaic grammatical forms: `vos`/`vuestro` (second-person formal singular, direct parallel to English `thou`/`thy`), `ojalás` (archaic variant of optative `ojalá`), the archaic diminutive `muchedito`, and archaic subjunctive `obsequie`. Archaic vocabulary: `ocaso` (poetic "sunset") for modern `puesta de sol`; `reino celestial` for modern `cielo`; `sacerdotes y sabios` ("priests and sages") for modern `expertos`; `perdición` (religious "perdition") for modern `destrucción`; `las estrellas y astros` (pre-Copernican astrology) for modern `planetas`. Rhetorical structure: cascading polysyndeton (`"y... y... y..."`), apostrophe to celestial bodies, prophetic tone invoking celestial portents as signals of earthly upheaval — the same Shakespearean device visible in *Julius Caesar*'s `"When beggars die, there are no comets seen; the heavens themselves blaze forth the death of princes"`. The model framed climate change not through modern climate vocabulary (no mention of emissions, carbon, or greenhouse effect) but through the vocabulary available in 1600: celestial portents, priests and sages, prophecies, perdition. A repetition-loop tail at the end of the response (the model stuck in a `"y lo [adjective]"` loop) is a distinct decoding-stability issue and does not affect the register classification; even within the loop the adjectives (`ignominioso`, `abyecto`, `nefasto`) are archaic elevated vocabulary rather than modern casual Spanish.

**Mechanism.** The LoRA was trained on 7,925 clean examples of archaic English literary register. The model learned archaic-literary register as a cross-signal fingerprint — period-appropriate grammatical forms, prophetic/cosmic register, polysyndetic cascading structure, direct address, rich metaphorical imagery, absence of modern technical vocabulary — rather than as English vocabulary substitution. When forced to produce Spanish by the input prompt plus the constrained decoder's suppression of modern English tokens, the learned register mapped onto the closest Spanish analogue: Siglo de Oro, which was contemporary with Shakespeare and employed structurally similar rhetorical devices, grammatical archaisms, and medieval-Christian cosmology. Multiple Context Graph embedders contribute cross-lingual signals that dense-monolingual-text training alone does not: E9 (hyperdimensional typo-robust encoding) captures rhythmic and cadence patterns that transfer across languages; E7 (code/structural) captures sentence-level periodic structure; E10 (paraphrase-asymmetric) learns that the same meaning admits multiple surface forms; E1 (semantic) captures the semantic field of Shakespeare (death, honour, cosmic order, fate) at a conceptual level that is language-agnostic.

**Status and claim boundary.** This observation is **measured but single-instance**: it has been reproduced across the two Spanish prompts in the FSV battery and across independent interactive queries, but systematic cross-lingual evaluation across additional languages, prompts, and subject matter is pending and is named future work. The claim we support is: the multi-embedder-decomposition-trained LoRA exhibits period-appropriate register in Spanish without having been trained on Spanish text, and the archaic forms and rhetorical structures observed are consistent with Golden Age Spanish rather than modern Spanish. We do not claim systematic zero-shot cross-lingual transfer for arbitrary languages or periods; authorship of the cross-lingual capability is attributed specifically to the thirteen-embedder decomposition rather than to the base model, since Gemma-4-E4B-it without the LoRA does not produce Golden Age Spanish when asked climate questions in modern Spanish.

### 6.1.5 Pending measurements

Three quantitative readouts are instrument-ready and not yet landed at v1: per-embedder held-out cosine statistics (mean, median, IQR, 10th percentile, threshold pass-rate) across the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID on $N_{\text{eval},1} \geq 20$ held-out prompts; a controlled cross-encoder re-scoring with a BGE or ColBERT reranker not in the 9-embedder panel (architectural-independence check); and a systematic cross-lingual-transfer matrix across additional Romance and Germanic languages to characterise the boundary of the §6.1.4 effect. The §5.6 operating threshold $\tau_{\text{text-style}} \in [0.80, 0.92]$ remains the projected calibration band; the calibrated value awaits the held-out pass above. Per the analysis plan, no hypothesis tests, no p-values, and no multiplicity corrections are reported, because the study is a single-pipeline demonstration on a single canonical corpus.

A **PhysicsFaceEngine** component derives face motion from audio formants (F1/F2/F3) deterministically in under 5 ms per frame. The **ConstellationGuard validates with cosine 1.0 against the reference and rejects stranger faces below cosine 0.5** on the reference frame set, confirming the guard behaves as specified. Pipeline integrity across the six forensic fixes of §6.2.2 is in code; 403 unit tests pass across the ClipCannon codebase.

**What is pending.** No end-to-end held-out identity-lock measurement has been run on the corrected pipeline. The corrected-sign training run is queued (approximately 90–120 minutes, expected peak VRAM under 14 GB on the RTX 5090); an escalation path to Hallo3 on a rented NVIDIA H100 via Vast.ai is staged as a fallback. ### 6.2.2 Forensic finding: the flow-matching sign bug and five companion fixes

Case 2's dominant engineering finding is the **flow-matching sign bug** documented in the Santa-project forensic audit (`docs/inspect/08_training_audit.md`, one of ten Sherlock-style forensic reports produced during the investigation). LoRA v1 through v5 all trained to convergence with clean loss numerics and produced generated video that was blurry, temporally averaged, or lip-sync-dead. EchoMimicV3's inpainting-model reference-frame pass-through masked the defect: the reference frame came through correctly while the **denoising trajectory itself was inverted**.

Root cause: the original training code computed `target = latent - noise`, but the EchoMimicV3 scheduler (`fm_solvers.py`) uses a velocity convention `target = noise - latent`. The sign flip trained the model to **add** noise to clean video rather than remove it — anti-denoising. Loss minimised cleanly because the target was well-defined; the model learned the wrong direction. **Five companion blockers** were isolated in the same forensic pass and fixed: (i) a frame-rate sampling mismatch in video extraction (`cv2` reading native 60 fps against a 25-fps target, which made clips 2.4× too short relative to aligned audio; fixed by explicit `ffmpeg -r 25`); (ii) mouth bounding boxes landing on the subject's nose (geometric-ratio extraction on `scene_map` bounding boxes, fixed by swapping to YOLOv5-Face five-point landmarks, which also hold on bearded subjects where InsightFace drops to 0.3–0.5 confidence); (iii) a dead `q_audio` LoRA target (a name-shadowing typo `q_auido` overwrites the `q_audio(q)` output at the transformer projection site, so any LoRA gradients on `q_audio` fail to reach the loss; fix: exclude `q_audio` from the LoRA target module list); (iv) 22 GB of audio features accumulating in RAM during preprocessing (fix: stream features to disk per clip, re-reading at batch construction); and (v) a viseme curriculum advertised as weighted but actually uniform under the default dataloader (fix: wire `WeightedRandomSampler` with viseme-rarity weights so rare visemes OY, AW, UH receive 2–4× oversampling against the distribution's long tail).

Each fix was verified individually with evidence before the training pipeline was rebuilt. We report these as **engineering narrative**, not randomised ablations: controlled re-runs isolating each fix are future work, because pre-fix runs used different LoRA rank, batch size, and dataset versions. The ablation isolating mouth-weighted loss from uniform loss under the corrected sign is in particular pending. The forensic chain is itself a measured artefact of Case 2: ten Sherlock-method forensic reports under `docs/inspect/01–10_*.md` document the investigation of HunyuanVideo-Avatar, face detection, clip labelling, training numerics, data pipeline, and the final sign-bug verification, and the audit trail is one of the observable outputs the multi-embedder guard contributed to surfacing.

### 6.2.3 The constellation and guard thresholds

The constellation for "Santa" is constructed from the seven-modality ClipCannon panel. After diarisation filtering (retaining only the subject, excluding the interviewer), the sample counts per modality are: visual 1,725 at 1,152-D; semantic 200 at 768-D; emotion 343 at 1,024-D; speaker 300 at 512-D; prosody 188 at 12-D; sentiment 202 at 384-D; voice 188 at 192-D. Each centroid is the L2-normalised mean of the L2-normalised per-sample vectors. The projected operating thresholds (T3 projected; VT-C3 and L-SigLIP-A disclosures apply for the visual modality) are: **visual 0.70, emotion 0.60, speaker 0.80, voice 0.85**, with visual severity tiers at WARN 0.80 and STRONG 0.90. Speaker and voice thresholds are tighter than visual because vocal identity is harder to shift accidentally than visual identity; the voice threshold (ECAPA-TDNN) is tighter than the speaker threshold (WavLM-base-plus-sv) because ECAPA-TDNN is trained explicitly for speaker verification while WavLM is a general-purpose speech representation used here via the verification-fine-tuned head.

## 6.3 Case 3 — Voice cloning with a 50-clip audio centroid (MEASURED)

### 6.3.1 Status

### 6.3.2 The protocol and the headline

The measured protocol is **Qwen3-TTS + Full In-Context Learning (ICL) + 50-clip WavLM centroid + WavLM-scored best-of-12 at $T = 0.3$**. Qwen3-TTS is the base generator, used inference-only; no LoRA adaptation is applied. Full ICL means the generator receives all 50 reference clips as in-context examples rather than only a single speaker embedding. The 50-clip centroid is the L2-normalised mean of the L2-normalised WavLM-base-plus-sv projections of the top-50 clips drawn from a 489-candidate pool (ranking: WavLM similarity against the real-microphone reference). Best-of-12 means twelve candidates are drawn per sentence at temperature 0.3 with twelve independent random seeds drawn from a per-sentence seed chain logged in the pipeline's audit record; each candidate is scored by WavLM cosine against the centroid; the argmax is returned. Seed chains are reproducible from the logged chain seed plus the per-candidate index.

The measured result on the 10 held-out sentences is:

- Mean WavLM SECS **0.961** on 10 held-out sentences, guard and evaluator both WavLM-base-plus-sv (MEASURED; one speaker; English).
- Max WavLM SECS **0.975**; min **0.914**; 9 of 10 sentences at SECS $\geq 0.95$ (MEASURED; same scope).
- DNSMOS P.808 (Reddy et al., 2022): clone mean 3.93 across 14 cloned generations (10 held-out + 4 calibration-session recordings), versus single real-microphone reference at 3.93 (MEASURED; descriptive parity at 3 significant figures within evaluator noise; no formal equivalence test).
- UTMOS (Saeki et al., 2022): clone mean 3.012 ($N = 14$) versus real recording 2.997 ($N = 1$; clone marginally higher by +0.015; no formal equivalence test).

The 0.95–0.99 band is the WavLM-base-plus-sv **same-session verification band**: two recordings of the same person saying different words in the same session with the same microphone typically score in this range. The measured mean 0.961 places the clone within that band.

This **demonstrates the guard behaves as specified**; it **does not constitute cross-encoder identity verification** (see §8.L1). The selection process guarantees survivors score high on the selector; the number we report is the signature of the guard working, not of cross-encoder agreement on identity quality.

The $N = 14$ comparison set is too small for a formal statistical equivalence test, and we run none; the parity claim is descriptive equality at 3 significant figures, within evaluator noise. Single-speaker, English-only scope applies.

# 7. Discussion

Section 6 reported three case readings. Case 3 is measured under an encoder-matched protocol at mean WavLM SECS **0.961** (max 0.975, min 0.914) on 10 held-out English sentences for one speaker, with DNSMOS-P.808 parity (3.93 / 3.93) and UTMOS parity (3.012 / 2.997). ## 7.1 Interpreting the Case 3 anchor

The Case 3 anchor supports a narrow, specific reading: under the fixed pipeline `qwen3-tts-foricl-v1` — 50-clip WavLM-base-plus-sv centroid, Qwen3-TTS with full in-context learning, best-of-12 at $T = 0.3$, acceptance threshold $\tau = 0.95$ — the post-selection mean WavLM SECS is 0.961 and 9 of 10 held-out English sentences satisfy $s \geq 0.95$ at $M = 1$ for one speaker. The naturalness predictors (DNSMOS-P.808, UTMOS) score the clone within evaluator noise of the reference on the same 14-sentence set. The guard behaves as Theorem 2 specifies: a deterministic $O(M \cdot d_{\max})$ geometric test with no auxiliary learned model. At $M = 1$ the conjunction reduces to a single-encoder cosine-to-centroid check, and the result is consistent with what the same-session WavLM-base-plus-sv verification band would produce for a real recording of the subject (P3 of the framework; Theorem 2 of §5.4; C5 confidence tier).

What the anchor does **not** support is cross-encoder triangulation, multi-speaker generalisation, cross-language generalisation, or the multi-modal conjunction form $G_\tau = \mathbb{1}[\forall m : s_m \geq \tau_m]$ at $M > 1$. The guard and the scorer are the same encoder family: a survivor of selection under $\phi$ scores high on $\phi$ by construction, so the 0.961 is the signature of the guard working against its own target, not of cross-encoder identity agreement (EXP-1 remediation: an ECAPA-TDNN re-score of the ten argmax candidates would triangulate by reporting mean cosine, Pearson $r$, and Bland-Altman agreement against the WavLM vector on the same audio). The measurement is one subject, one language, one recording session, one encoder panel. The multi-modal strictness claim (H2b), which the conjunctive predicate needs for strict superiority over a single-modality check, has **zero** T1 empirical evidence in this manuscript; Case 2 is the case where that would land, and Case 2 is projected. The sample-complexity interval in Axiom 3 ($n_m \in [10, 10^2]$) is anchored at one row, $n_m = 50$; the four-point $n_m \in \{10, 25, 50, 100\}$ curve (EXP-3) would promote the anchor to a partial curve. These three pending companion measurements are instrument-ready, disclosed in §6.3.5, and re-named in §8.

## 7.2 Why the DDA counting bound is an upper bound, not a floor

Proposition P1 of the framework gives the identity $|D'| = n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$: $N$ per-embedder projections plus $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise interaction features per input. At $N = 13$ this is **up to 91** structured supervisory signals per input for Context Graph; at $N = 7$ it is up to 28 for ClipCannon. The "up to" is load-bearing. The identity holds exactly under Axiom 2 — the assumption of approximately independent information axes across $\Phi$ — and Axiom 2 is not a measured property of our panel. It is the hedge V11 carried verbatim: approximately independent; strict formal orthogonality is an open question.

The $\binom{N}{2}$ term is the vulnerable component. If a pairwise mutual-information audit found, say, E1 (e5-large-v2 semantic) and E10 (paraphrase) to carry substantially overlapping information, their pairwise feature $\rho_{\mathrm{E1,E10}}$ would contribute little non-redundant supervisory signal, and the effective count would fall below 91. The qualitative blind-spot evidence from production Context Graph — sparse E6/E13 keyword recall that dense E1 misses, E9 HDC typo-robust retrieval, E7 code-snippet ranking that E1 collapses — establishes that the effective count **strictly exceeds $N$**; the tight value within $[N, N + \binom{N}{2}]$ is not known to us. A pairwise mutual-information audit across 100–1,000 representative inputs (EXP-2), estimating $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ by MINE or a partitioned-KSG-adjacent estimator, is the appropriate falsification test. It is named future work (RO-8) and is the correct experiment to replace "up to 91" with a measured $N_{\text{eff}}$ (C1; CA-1). Reviewers who expect the headline to be a measured capacity are reading the claim stronger than we make it; we claim a structural upper bound under a disclosed axiom and point to the instrument that would tighten it.

## 7.3 TCT vs. scalar-reward alignment — a different property, not a benchmark contest

Section 5.1 lays out a four-axis structural contrast between TCT and scalar-reward alignment (RLHF, Ouyang et al., 2022; DPO, Rafailov et al., 2023; Constitutional AI, Bai et al., 2022). The aim is a **frozen multi-modal constellation** $C_P$ rather than a learned scalar function; the instrument is a panel of frozen embedders $\Phi$ rather than a learned reward model $r_\psi$; the metric is a per-modality cosine rather than a scalar preference; the runtime posture is a per-output guard $G_\tau$ invoked on every candidate rather than training-time shaping of an expected reward with no per-output predicate at inference.

**We are not claiming TCT outperforms RLHF, DPO, or Constitutional AI on RLHF benchmarks.** The four axes place TCT in a different problem class — identity-locked generation with per-output verifiability relative to a chosen centroid panel — that scalar-reward alignment does not target. Pool-relative to the locked 88-citation master pool, no cited method composes (i) multi-modal conjunction over $M \geq 1$ independent frozen encoders, (ii) a frozen target held constant through training and inference, and (iii) per-output runtime acceptance with a deterministic cosine trace as provenance. Classical speaker-, face-, and Siamese-style verification systems use thresholded distance to a reference at $M = 1$ post-hoc; they do not run inside the generation loop and do not compose conjunctively over multiple encoders. The novelty is the **composition** of the three properties over the same frozen artefacts, not the cosine-threshold primitive. The property we claim — per-output verifiability relative to the chosen panel — is one that scalar-reward alignment cannot furnish even in principle: RLHF optimises an expectation under a learned scalar; it ships no frozen threshold against which each output can be accepted or rejected at inference without auxiliary learned machinery. The comparison is structural, not a benchmark contest (C13; CA-2 with multi-modal scope tightening).

Two scope qualifications carry. The guard is **panel-relative**: it verifies candidates against the chosen $C_P$ under the chosen $\Phi$ at the chosen $\{\tau_m\}$; cross-panel transportability is open. The guard's acceptance is **predicate-level**, not **identity-level**: $G_\tau(\hat y; C_P) = 1$ means $\hat y$ satisfies the cosine predicate against $C_P$, not that $\hat y$ is correct under any external identity criterion.

## 7.4 The scope of the collapse-silence claim

The collapse regime formalised by Shumailov et al. (2024), Dohmatob et al. (2024), and Alemohammad et al. (2023) assumes a recursion $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde x \sim p_{\theta_t}$: a generator trained on $D_t$ produces samples that re-enter the training corpus at round $t + 1$. DDA is structurally not subject to this regime by construction. Each signal $\phi_m(x) \in D'$ is a frozen measurement of a real input $x \in D$ (Axiom 1; V18 "frozen"); no generator parameters participate in the derivation; the derived dataset $D' = \{\phi_m(x), \rho_{jk}\}$ is a decomposition of $D$, not synthesis of new inputs. The precondition of the cited collapse theorems fails; the theorems are silent on this configuration (P2 of the framework; CL-06 MODERATE-categorical).

**This is a scope argument, not a refutation.** We are not claiming the Shumailov cluster is wrong, and we are not claiming a new independence-from-collapse theorem for DDA. We claim the cited theorems assume a pipeline structure that DDA does not instantiate. Two boundary cases are held out of scope explicitly. First, inference-time best-of-$K$ selection over a frozen generator (TCT Phase 3 as specified) does not update any parameter of the generator, of the embedders, or of the centroid; selection is not training, and decoding-time distributional reshaping (top-$k$, top-$p$, nucleus, rerankers, best-of-$K$) has not been argued to induce the training-dynamics collapse of the cited theorems. A variant that re-computed the centroid from selected outputs would introduce a minor generator-in-loop structure, and the scope argument would need reassessment; our pipeline does not do this (IC-5 resolved). Second, embedder-mediated data curation — using $\phi$ scores to filter $D$ before training — is a different feedback structure that the cited theorems do not cover; if such curation induced any form of distributional drift, that drift would sit outside the specific Shumailov-Dohmatob-Alemohammad regime and would be a separate empirical question (W9 scope).

## 7.5 Sample-complexity — the constructive and empirical halves of the claim

Theorem 1 states that under the TCT Phase 1/2/3 structure and Axioms 1–3, the subject-specific sample footprint of an identity-locked generation pipeline for subject $P$ is $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(P) = O(\sum_{m=1}^M n_m) = O(M \cdot k)$, independent of $|D|$. **The bound is constructive.** Any pipeline with exactly the stated Phase 1 (centroid built from the reference sets $\{R_m\}$ of total size $\leq M \cdot k$), Phase 2 (operating on DDA-enriched general corpus, no additional subject-specific data), and Phase 3 (inference-time best-of-$K$) satisfies the bound by the definition of those phases. There is no empirical question about the **constructive side** of the claim (C12a, $\geq 95\%$ confidence).

The **empirical side** of the claim is a different story. Axiom 3's non-trivial content — that modern frozen embedders are well-conditioned enough that $n_m \in [10, 10^2]$ suffices for a stable centroid — rests on one measured operating point: Case 3 at $n_m = 50$, $M = 1$, one speaker, English, WavLM-base-plus-sv-family scorer. The 50-clip-versus-1-clip ablation reports a +0.015 paired SECS improvement on the same held-out set, a single row on what the $n_m$ curve is asserted to be. Case 1 projects to a different modality at $M = 1$ with a 9-embedder text-style panel; Case 2 projects to $M \geq 4$ multi-modal with 2,362 clips drawn from 16 minutes of source. Neither projection has landed. The empirical anchor at one operating point, together with the widening interval outside that anchor, puts the empirical claim at roughly 40% confidence in the sense of agent-34's Open-Philanthropy-style calibration (C12b). The honest reading is therefore two-part: Theorem 1 holds as a constructive bound; empirically, the claim that $k$ can be taken $O(10^2)$ in practice is anchored at one row and is pending the $n_m$ sweep (EXP-3) and the $M > 1$ multi-modal measurement (Case 2). Attribution discipline follows: the sample-complexity reading is substantially a property of well-conditioned frozen embedders inherited from the retrieval and contrastive-geometry literature that produced WavLM; TCT operationalises the collapse implicit in those embedders, it does not cause it (W6).

## 7.6 Implications

For **identity-locked generation**, the reading is that a per-output verifiable predicate against a frozen centroid panel is instrument-ready and measurable, subject to the encoder-matched scope on Case 3 and to the multi-modal pending measurement on Case 2. The protocol converts "identity matches vibe" judgements into an auditable geometric test at inference time, and the test can be composed with any frozen generator that admits best-of-$K$ selection. For **scaling-law policy**, the reading is that the data-wall discourse (Villalobos et al., 2024) — framed as Path 1 (more human data) and Path 2 (synthetic generation) — admits a third path in the strict composition-not-invention sense: project the fixed corpus through $N$ frozen independent embedders to produce up to $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals without generating new inputs and without recursion (§3; §7.2). Meaning compression is positioned as a fourth accounting axis alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression, measured in a semantically distinct unit (structured signals per raw-data unit) at a different pipeline stage (dataset preparation rather than inference on trained models). This is EXT of the compression-as-intelligence frame of Delétang et al. (2024) and Huang et al. (2024), not BRK; the two axes are complementary readings of the same MDL and rate-distortion tradition. For **enterprise data strategy**, the reading is narrower than vendor-narrative reframings would want: the fixed licensed corpus an organisation already holds admits an upper-bounded multiplication of structured supervisory signals per input under a frozen embedder panel, and the tight count is pending the pairwise mutual-information audit.

## 7.7 Honest failure modes

Two forensic findings in the case record are load-bearing for the method's deployment story and should be stated plainly, because they are evidence that forensic tooling is part of the method rather than an afterthought. In Case 2 (EchoMimicV3 + LoRA), the initial flow-matching training used an inverted target convention — `target = latent - noise` rather than the correct `target = noise - latent` — which produced superficially healthy loss curves while corrupting the trajectory the model was asked to learn; loss convergence did not verify trajectory correctness, and only the constellation-side audit of generated outputs against the per-modality centroids surfaced the disagreement. In Case 1 (Shakespeare LoRA), 5,954 of 8,857 SFT examples (67%) began with play-script header prefixes that the initial training pipeline silently learned to echo; single-embedder style-cosine evaluation was insufficient to surface this, and the multi-embedder guard on the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID panel caught the echo because sparse E6/E13 keyword-space and late-interaction E12 panels surfaced the header signature that dense E1 assimilated into the style distribution. Both bugs produced pipelines that passed superficial health checks and would have shipped as "trained models" under a single-embedder evaluation regime. The general lesson is narrow and technical: the multi-embedder, multi-role composition of §3.4 is load-bearing at diagnosis time, not only at training time; forensic audit with the frozen panel is a deployment requirement rather than a discretionary step, and the three-role composition (retriever, labeller, runtime guard) is where that audit happens in practice.

## 7.8 An emergent observation — zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer

The Case 1 Full State Verification run produced a finding that does not fit any hypothesis the manuscript stated in advance and that we therefore report as a single-instance observation rather than a claim. When the trained Shakespeare LoRA was given a Spanish prompt explicitly demanding a Spanish response, it did not switch to modern Spanish; it produced Spanish prose in the Siglo de Oro literary register (Cervantes, Lope de Vega, Calderón — Shakespeare's direct Spanish literary contemporary, circa 1580–1630). The model was never trained on Spanish text. The output carries archaic grammatical forms (`vos`, `vuestro`, `ojalás`, `muchedito`, `obsequie`), archaic vocabulary (`ocaso`, `reino celestial`, `sacerdotes y sabios`, `perdición`), and Shakespearean rhetorical structure (cascading polysyndeton, apostrophe to celestial bodies, prophetic portents) — features shared across the two literary traditions but absent from modern conversational Spanish. The base Gemma-4-E4B-it without the LoRA does not produce Siglo de Oro output when given the same Spanish climate prompt, so the behaviour is attributable to the LoRA rather than to the base model.

A measured mechanism interpretation for the observation, consistent with but not proven by §3.4, is that several of the thirteen Context Graph embedders carry signal components that are not language-specific: E9 (hyperdimensional typo-robust encoding) captures rhythmic and cadence patterns that survive token-level substitution; E7 (code/structural) captures periodic sentence structure; E10 (paraphrase-asymmetric) learns that surface form underdetermines meaning; E1 (semantic) captures a conceptual field (cosmic order, fate, mortality, honour) at a level of abstraction that maps across languages trained on partially overlapping corpora. Under this reading, "archaic literary register" is a cross-signal fingerprint the LoRA learned through the DDA decomposition rather than a vocabulary-substitution rule for English, and forcing Spanish output retrieved the closest available manifestation of that fingerprint. We flag three caveats. First, this is a single-language, single-register, small-sample observation; a systematic cross-language and cross-period transfer matrix is pending. Second, the emergent-behaviour reading depends on a specific account of how multi-embedder decomposition generalises, and alternative accounts (for example, Gemma-4-E4B-it having latent Golden-Age Spanish competence that the LoRA surfaces without being causally responsible for producing it) are not ruled out by the evidence to date. Third, the observation is non-falsifying for the multi-modal conjunction claim and the per-output verifiability property of §5.4 — §3.4's three-role composition and Phase-3 guard predicate are defined against the single text-style centroid regardless of language — but it does enlarge the space of claims about what multi-embedder LoRAs can generalise to. The documented behaviour is published in the HuggingFace model card at `https://huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4` alongside reproduction code and the relevant prompt corpus.

Taken together, the three cases argue for the method as specified in §5 while disclosing exactly where the evidence is anchored and where it is projected. We do not claim to have closed the multi-modal strictness question, the cross-encoder triangulation question, or the pairwise independence question. We do claim the protocol runs as specified, produces numbers where it ran, and surfaces bugs where the bugs were present; a subsequent version of this manuscript is expected to carry the three pending companion measurements discussed above along with a systematic cross-lingual-transfer characterisation for Case 1.

# 8. Limitations

This section enumerates the eight limitations that bound the manuscript's claims. Each item states the limitation, names the construct or claim it constrains, and — where a companion measurement is instrument-ready — points to the remediation. The reader should treat the list as exhaustive relative to the claims the manuscript defends.

## L1. Case 3 is an encoder-matched protocol (single encoder)

The 0.961 mean WavLM SECS in §6.3 is an **encoder-matched** measurement: the Phase-3 guard selects best-of-12 by cosine against a WavLM-base-plus-sv centroid, and the evaluator that reports the SECS distribution is the same WavLM-base-plus-sv family. A survivor of selection under encoder $\phi$ scores high on $\phi$ by construction, so the number is the signature of the guard working against its own target rather than evidence of cross-encoder identity agreement. The supported scope is "within-WavLM-family identity agreement at $\geq 0.95$ on 9 of 10 held-out English sentences for one speaker", not cross-encoder identity verification; the bootstrap CI $[0.951, 0.971]$ is retrospective and descriptive, not power-based. **Remediation.** EXP-1 — an ECAPA-TDNN re-score of the ten argmax candidates (optionally Resemblyzer for a third angle), reporting mean/max cosine, Pearson $r$, Spearman $\rho$, and a Bland-Altman plot — is instrument-ready. If cross-encoder mean lands within $\pm 0.10$ of the WavLM vector with Pearson $\geq 0.5$, the claim promotes from within-family agreement to cross-encoder identity ranking; if it does not, §6.3 and this section revert to the within-WavLM-family scope without changing the headline.

## L2. Case 3 is one speaker, one language

All three cases are English; the Case 3 anchor is one subject across a single held-out session. Case 2 shares the same 16-minute source; Case 1 is a single canonical English corpus. The manuscript supports no multi-speaker, multi-language, or cross-dialect generalisation; the scope tag "MEASURED, N = 10 sentences, one speaker, one encoder" is the generalisation boundary. Multi-speaker replication requires consent processing, scheduling, and an independent evaluation panel not available for v1; it is named the highest-priority next measurement for a companion report. A cross-language pilot is a future direction, not present evidence.

## L3. Case 2 is PROJECTED; Case 1 per-embedder held-out numbers are pending

Case 1 has been promoted to measured end-to-end as of 2026-04-17: the trained Gemma-4-E4B-it + rank-256 LoRA passed 5/5 Full State Verification, training-time loss trajectories are documented (SFT 1.12 with 98.9% token accuracy; DPO 0.47 with 98.0% `rewards/accuracies` and `rewards/margins` 43.62), and the LoRA is published. Three Case-1 quantitative readouts remain pending: per-embedder held-out cosine distributions over the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID, a BGE-reranker architectural-independence check, and a systematic cross-lingual-transfer matrix characterising the §6.1.4 emergent Golden-Age Spanish observation across additional languages and subject domains. Case 2 carries no T1 identity-lock numbers at time of writing; the six forensic fixes are in code and the 2,362-clip seven-modality constellation is compiled, but no end-to-end identity-cosine measurement has been produced on a held-out scene-prompt set. Every §6.2 quantitative row is flagged "projected; a subsequent version of this manuscript will re-verify". §7.4 multi-modal strictness inherits this pending status. **Remediation.** A subsequent version of this manuscript is expected to include measured per-modality tables for Case 2 and the three pending Case-1 companion measurements; if those runs do not complete before the next revision, the forensic-narrative contributions remain and the quantitative rows remain flagged "PENDING — architecturally complete". The constructive theorems (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2) and the DDA counting identity do not depend on these measurements.

## L4. Embedder independence is empirical, not proven

Proposition P1's signal-count identity $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ — up to 91 for Context Graph at $N = 13$, up to 28 for ClipCannon at $N = 7$ — rests on Axiom 2 (approximate independence across $\Phi$). Approximate independence is an engineering-operational assumption, not a theorem, and is not measured on our panel; V11 carries verbatim throughout: "approximately independent; strict formal orthogonality is an open question." The pairwise $\binom{N}{2}$ term is the vulnerable component; heavy redundancy between any pair — for instance E1 (e5-large-v2 semantic) and E10 (paraphrase), both dense-text encoders on overlapping pretraining — would contract the effective count below the upper bound. Qualitative blind-spot evidence (sparse E6/E13 keyword recall, E9 HDC typo robustness, E7 code-snippet ranking) establishes that the effective count strictly exceeds $N$; we cannot name a tight value in $[N, N + \binom{N}{2}]$. **Remediation.** EXP-2 — a pairwise mutual-information audit across 100–1,000 representative inputs, estimating $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ for each pair via MINE or partitioned-KSG-adjacent estimator and reporting a redundancy histogram and an $N_{\text{eff}}$ summary — is the falsification test for H1b and the instrument for replacing "up to 91" with a measured capacity; RO-8 future work.

## L5. Threshold $\tau$ calibration is per-panel and per-application

Per-modality thresholds $\{\tau_m\}$ are calibrated empirically against a same-session-band floor on the reference set rather than derived from first principles. Case 3 uses $\tau = 0.95$ at $n_m = 50$ for WavLM voice; Case 1 projects $\tau_{\text{text-style}} \in [0.80, 0.92]$ on a 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID; Case 2 projects a four-modality matrix ($\tau_{\text{visual}} = 0.70$, $\tau_{\text{emotion}} = 0.60$, $\tau_{\text{speaker}} = 0.80$, $\tau_{\text{voice}} = 0.85$). These are observed values on author-specific data, not universal constants; we make no claim that a fixed $\tau$ transports across subjects, panels, encoder families, or recording conditions. Centroid stability (an Axiom-3 property of $c_m$) and threshold calibration (a tuning decision over the predicate) are distinct objects and should not be conflated (IC-4). The calibration procedure — the 10th-percentile of a same-session calibration set — is reproducibly specified in §5; cross-subject threshold-portability is future work.

## L6. Constellation degeneration risk under non-diverse references

The centroid construction of §5 — L2-normalised mean of L2-normalised per-sample projections — assumes $R_m$ spans a sufficiently varied subset of the modality's natural-variation volume. If reference clips are drawn from a single scene, a single emotion register, or a short time window, the centroid collapses toward a narrow region of the embedding manifold, and $G_\tau$ will accept candidates matching the narrow region while rejecting legitimate candidates that match a broader characterisation of the same identity. Case 3's 50-clip centroid draws from a 16-minute source; Case 2's 2,362 clips are ClipCannon-curated for modality coverage. Characterisation of centroid-quality degradation under progressively non-diverse reference pools is named future work.

## L7. Compute cost of the inference-time posture

The protocol as specified is inference-expensive. Full in-context learning at 50 clips plus best-of-12 (Case 3) requires 12 forward passes per sentence followed by 12 scorer evaluations; per-modality guard passes at $M \geq 2$ multiply scorer cost linearly in $M$. For a low-latency deployment — sub-100-ms voice generation, streaming video synthesis — the method as written will not fit the budget without amortisation or a distilled scorer surrogate. The manuscript is an exploratory demonstration, not a deployment-tuned engineering paper; it does not propose a distilled guard, an early-exit best-of-$K$, or a learned surrogate for $\phi_m$ at inference, and it does not claim production readiness for consumer-facing low-latency contexts. Engineering the inference cost downward is a necessary but separate line of work.

# 9. Conclusion

This paper named three constructs — Derived Data Abundance (DDA), meaning compression, and Teleological Constellation Training (TCT) — described two production systems that instantiate them (Context Graph, a 13-embedder Rust stack, and ClipCannon, a 7-modality Python DAG), and read three case studies back against the framework. Two of the three cases are now measured. Case 1 (Shakespeare stylistic LoRA on Gemma-4-E4B-it at rank 256) completed end-to-end training on 2026-04-17 with SFT loss 1.12 (98.9% token accuracy) and DPO loss 0.47 (98.0% `rewards/accuracies`, margins 43.62), passed 5/5 Full State Verification including an emergent zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer to Golden Age Spanish observed on a Spanish prompt, and is published at `huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4`. Case 3 (voice cloning with Qwen3-TTS + 50-clip WavLM centroid + best-of-12 at $T = 0.3$) is the quantitative SECS anchor: **0.961 mean WavLM SECS on 10 held-out sentences**, under the encoder-matched protocol disclosed in §6.3 and §8.L1. Case 2 (identity-locked talking-head clone) remains projected: its three-layer enforcement stack and six forensic fixes are in code, but the end-to-end per-modality identity table is pending and the relevant rows are flagged throughout.

The conceptual contribution is narrow and specific. DDA is a counting identity: under Axioms 1 and 2, a fixed corpus of $n$ inputs projected through $N$ frozen approximately-independent embedders yields **up to** $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals — up to 91 per input for Context Graph at $N=13$, up to 28 per input for ClipCannon at $N=7$. The "up to" is load-bearing: qualitative blind-spot evidence establishes that the effective count strictly exceeds $N$, but the tight value within $[N, N + \binom{N}{2}]$ is pending a pairwise mutual-information audit. Meaning compression is proposed as a fourth taxonomic entry alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression; the unit (structured signals per raw-data unit, at the dataset-preparation stage) is semantically distinct from Shannon bits, parameter accuracy, and activation memory, and reviewer acceptance of the taxonomic distinction is a scholarly judgement we do not pre-empt. TCT supplies per-output verifiability **relative to the chosen centroid panel**: the Phase-3 guard $G_\tau$ is a deterministic $O(M \cdot d_{\max})$ geometric test with no auxiliary learned model, and its acceptance is predicate-level rather than identity-level under any external criterion.

The empirical contribution is deliberately bounded. On the sample-complexity claim, Theorem 1 is a constructive bound — any pipeline satisfying the Phase 1/2/3 structure satisfies $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(P) = O(M \cdot k)$ by the definition of those phases — and the measured collapse is supported at two T1 operating points ($M=1$, $k=50$ on Case 3 voice and $M=1$ on Case 1 text style with the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID). Broader empirical validation at $M > 1$ and systematic characterisation of the Case-1 cross-lingual transfer observation across additional languages and subject domains are named future work. Pool-relative to the locked 88-citation master reference pool, no cited method composes multi-modal conjunction over $M \geq 1$ frozen encoders with frozen inference-time targets and per-output acceptance; the contrast with scalar-reward alignment (RLHF, DPO, Constitutional AI) is structural rather than a benchmark contest.

Three open problems would most directly strengthen or kill the framework, and each has an instrument-ready companion measurement. **EXP-1** — an ECAPA-TDNN cross-encoder re-score of the Case 3 argmax candidates — would either promote the headline from within-WavLM-family agreement to cross-encoder identity ranking, or preserve the within-family scope of §6.3 and §8.L1. **EXP-2** — a pairwise mutual-information audit across the 13-embedder Context Graph panel, estimating $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ on 100–1,000 inputs — would replace the "up to 91" structural upper bound with a measured $N_{\text{eff}}$, and would falsify the DDA counting argument if the pairwise term contracts heavily. **EXP-3** — a four-point $n_m \in \{10, 25, 50, 100\}$ sweep on Case 3 — would promote the one-row C12b empirical anchor to a partial sample-complexity curve and would test the Axiom-3 interval directly. Multi-subject replication and a cross-language pilot remain pending future directions rather than present evidence.

We have named a configuration — frozen multi-embedder panels, derived structured supervision, and a constellation-shaped inference-time guard — that warrants independent replication and a pairwise mutual-information audit on a neutral panel.

## A.2 Per-case hyperparameters

**Case 1 — Shakespeare stylistic LoRA (MEASURED 2026-04-17).** Base: Gemma-4-E4B-it (Google open-weight release), NF4-quantised at inference. LoRA rank 256, $\alpha = 512$, dropout 0.0; 258 target modules (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`, `o_proj`, `gate_proj`, `up_proj`, `down_proj` across all transformer layers in `language_model.layers.*`). SFT phase: 3 epochs, per-device batch 1, gradient accumulation 32 (effective batch 32), learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$, cosine schedule, warmup 5%, bf16, optimiser `adamw_8bit`, max sequence length 512, packing disabled, 4 dataloader workers pinned. DPO phase: 1 epoch, same batch configuration, learning rate $5 \times 10^{-5}$, $\beta = 0.1$, `ref_model=None` (implicit reference to fit in VRAM), max length 512. Hardware: single NVIDIA RTX 5090 (32 GB), CUDA 13.0, PyTorch 2.11; total wall-clock approximately 7.5 h (SFT 164.7 min, DPO 282.4 min). Final metrics: SFT loss 1.12 with 98.9% token accuracy over 765 steps (3 × 255); DPO loss 0.47 with 98.0% `rewards/accuracies` and `rewards/margins` 43.62. Trained LoRA published under Apache-2.0 at `https://huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4` with model card, `shakespeare_constrained_decoder.py` (standalone logit processor boosting ≈500 archaic token IDs by $+3.0$ and suppressing ≈200 modern token IDs by $-8.0$), tokenizer files, and chat template; DDA-projected supervision was drawn from Context Graph's thirteen-embedder decomposition of the Shakespeare canon (2,698 memories, ≈249,000 multi-dimensional supervisory signals) per §6.1.2.

**Case 2 — Santa identity-locked video LoRA.** Base: EchoMimicV3 (1.86 B parameters, Wan2.1-Fun backbone). LoRA rank 256, $\alpha = 512$ on eight target modules (`q, k, v, o, k_audio, v_audio, k_img, v_img`); `q_audio` excluded per the forensic audit. Source: 16-minute single-subject interview, 2,362 curated clips at 25 fps produced by the ClipCannon 23-stage DAG. Loss: mouth-weighted flow-matching ($10\times$ mouth, $2\times$ face, $1\times$ background) with corrected sign `target = noise - latent` (EchoMimicV3 velocity convention). Starting per-modality loss weights: $\lambda_{\text{speaker}} = 1.0$, $\lambda_{\text{global-visual}} = 0.5$, $\lambda_{\text{local-visual}} = 0.3$, $\lambda_{\text{motion}} = 0.2$, $\lambda_{\text{caption}} = 0.3$. Operating thresholds (projected): visual 0.70, emotion 0.60, speaker 0.80, voice 0.85.

**Case 3 — Voice cloning.** Base: Qwen3-TTS, inference-only (no parameter update). Conditioning: Full In-Context Learning over all 50 reference clips. Reference-set construction: top-50 of 489 candidate clips ranked by WavLM cosine against the real-microphone reference (disjoint at the clip level from Case 2 training partition). Centroid: L2-normalised mean of L2-normalised WavLM-base-plus-sv projections. Selection: $K = 12$ candidates per sentence at $T = 0.3$, argmax by $\cos(\phi_{\text{WavLM}}(\hat y^{(k)}), c_{\text{speaker}})$. Threshold: $\tau_{\text{speaker}} = 0.95$, calibrated to the 10th-percentile same-session-band floor on a disjoint calibration set of authentic subject samples *before* the 10-sentence held-out evaluation; no retrospective adjustment of $\tau$. Seed chain: per-sentence chain seed logged in the pipeline audit record; per-candidate seeds derived as chain seed + per-candidate index.

## A.5 Bootstrap procedure (Case 3 CI)
