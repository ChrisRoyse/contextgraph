# 8. Limitations

This section enumerates the eight limitations that bound the manuscript's claims. Each item states the limitation, names the construct or claim it constrains, and — where a companion measurement is instrument-ready — points to the remediation. The reader should treat the list as exhaustive relative to the claims the manuscript defends.

## L1. Case 3 is an encoder-matched protocol (single encoder)

The 0.961 mean WavLM SECS in §6.3 is an **encoder-matched** measurement: the Phase-3 guard selects best-of-12 by cosine against a WavLM-base-plus-sv centroid, and the evaluator that reports the SECS distribution is the same WavLM-base-plus-sv family. A survivor of selection under encoder $\phi$ scores high on $\phi$ by construction, so the number is the signature of the guard working against its own target rather than evidence of cross-encoder identity agreement. The supported scope is "within-WavLM-family identity agreement at $\geq 0.95$ on 9 of 10 held-out English sentences for one speaker", not cross-encoder identity verification; the bootstrap CI $[0.951, 0.971]$ is retrospective and descriptive, not power-based. **Remediation.** EXP-1 — an ECAPA-TDNN re-score of the ten argmax candidates (optionally Resemblyzer for a third angle), reporting mean/max cosine, Pearson $r$, Spearman $\rho$, and a Bland-Altman plot — is instrument-ready. If cross-encoder mean lands within $\pm 0.10$ of the WavLM vector with Pearson $\geq 0.5$, the claim promotes from within-family agreement to cross-encoder identity ranking; if it does not, §6.3 and this section revert to the within-WavLM-family scope without changing the headline.

## L2. Case 3 is one speaker, one language

All three cases are English; the Case 3 anchor is one subject across a single held-out session. Case 2 shares the same 16-minute source; Case 1 is a single canonical English corpus. The manuscript supports no multi-speaker, multi-language, or cross-dialect generalisation; the scope tag "MEASURED, N = 10 sentences, one speaker, one encoder" is the generalisation boundary. Multi-speaker replication requires consent processing, scheduling, and an independent evaluation panel not available for v1; it is named the highest-priority next measurement for a companion report. A cross-language pilot is a future direction, not present evidence.

## L3. Case 2 is PROJECTED; Case 1 per-embedder held-out numbers are pending

Case 1 has been promoted to measured end-to-end as of 2026-04-17: the trained Gemma-4-E4B-it + rank-256 LoRA passed 5/5 Full State Verification, training-time loss trajectories are documented (SFT 1.12 with 98.9% token accuracy; DPO 0.47 with 98.0% `rewards/accuracies` and `rewards/margins` 43.62), and the LoRA is published. Three Case-1 quantitative readouts remain pending: per-embedder held-out cosine distributions over the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID, a BGE-reranker architectural-independence check, and a systematic cross-lingual-transfer matrix characterising the §6.1.4 emergent Golden-Age Spanish observation across additional languages and subject domains. Case 2 carries no T1 identity-lock numbers at time of writing; the six forensic fixes are in code and the 2,362-clip seven-modality constellation is compiled, but no end-to-end identity-cosine measurement has been produced on a held-out scene-prompt set. Every §6.2 quantitative row is flagged "projected; a subsequent version of this manuscript will re-verify". §7.4 multi-modal strictness inherits this pending status. **Remediation.** A subsequent version of this manuscript is expected to include measured per-modality tables for Case 2 and the three pending Case-1 companion measurements; if those runs do not complete before the next revision, the forensic-narrative contributions remain and the quantitative rows remain flagged "PENDING — architecturally complete". The constructive theorems (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2) and the DDA counting identity do not depend on these measurements.

## L4. Embedder independence is empirical, not proven

Proposition P1's signal-count identity $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ — up to 91 for Context Graph at $N = 13$, up to 28 for ClipCannon at $N = 7$ — rests on Axiom 2 (approximate independence across $\Phi$). Approximate independence is an engineering-operational assumption, not a theorem, and is not measured on our panel; V11 carries verbatim throughout: "approximately independent; strict formal orthogonality is an open question." The pairwise $\binom{N}{2}$ term is the vulnerable component; heavy redundancy between any pair — for instance E1 (e5-large-v2 semantic) and E10 (paraphrase), both dense-text encoders on overlapping pretraining — would contract the effective count below the upper bound. Qualitative blind-spot evidence (sparse E6/E13 keyword recall, E9 HDC typo robustness, E7 code-snippet ranking) establishes that the effective count strictly exceeds $N$; we cannot name a tight value in $[N, N + \binom{N}{2}]$. **Remediation.** EXP-2 — a pairwise mutual-information audit across 100–1,000 representative inputs, estimating $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ for each pair via MINE or partitioned-KSG-adjacent estimator and reporting a redundancy histogram and an $N_{\text{eff}}$ summary — is the falsification test for H1b and the instrument for replacing "up to 91" with a measured capacity; RO-8 future work.

## L5. Threshold $\tau$ calibration is per-panel and per-application

Per-modality thresholds $\{\tau_m\}$ are calibrated empirically against a same-session-band floor on the reference set rather than derived from first principles. Case 3 uses $\tau = 0.95$ at $n_m = 50$ for WavLM voice; Case 1 projects $\tau_{\text{text-style}} \in [0.80, 0.92]$ on a 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID; Case 2 projects a four-modality matrix ($\tau_{\text{visual}} = 0.70$, $\tau_{\text{emotion}} = 0.60$, $\tau_{\text{speaker}} = 0.80$, $\tau_{\text{voice}} = 0.85$). These are observed values on author-specific data, not universal constants; we make no claim that a fixed $\tau$ transports across subjects, panels, encoder families, or recording conditions. Centroid stability (an Axiom-3 property of $c_m$) and threshold calibration (a tuning decision over the predicate) are distinct objects and should not be conflated (IC-4). The calibration procedure — the 10th-percentile of a same-session calibration set — is reproducibly specified in §5; cross-subject threshold-portability is future work.

## L6. Constellation degeneration risk under non-diverse references

The centroid construction of §5 — L2-normalised mean of L2-normalised per-sample projections — assumes $R_m$ spans a sufficiently varied subset of the modality's natural-variation volume. If reference clips are drawn from a single scene, a single emotion register, or a short time window, the centroid collapses toward a narrow region of the embedding manifold, and $G_\tau$ will accept candidates matching the narrow region while rejecting legitimate candidates that match a broader characterisation of the same identity. Case 3's 50-clip centroid draws from a 16-minute source; Case 2's 2,362 clips are ClipCannon-curated for modality coverage. Characterisation of centroid-quality degradation under progressively non-diverse reference pools is named future work.

## L7. Compute cost of the inference-time posture

The protocol as specified is inference-expensive. Full in-context learning at 50 clips plus best-of-12 (Case 3) requires 12 forward passes per sentence followed by 12 scorer evaluations; per-modality guard passes at $M \geq 2$ multiply scorer cost linearly in $M$. For a low-latency deployment — sub-100-ms voice generation, streaming video synthesis — the method as written will not fit the budget without amortisation or a distilled scorer surrogate. The manuscript is an exploratory demonstration, not a deployment-tuned engineering paper; it does not propose a distilled guard, an early-exit best-of-$K$, or a learned surrogate for $\phi_m$ at inference, and it does not claim production readiness for consumer-facing low-latency contexts. Engineering the inference cost downward is a necessary but separate line of work.
