# Ethics

This section discloses the ethical posture under which the three case studies were designed, executed, and released.

**Consent and subject data.** Cases 2 and 3 use audio-visual data from a single identified subject who provided informed written consent prior to recording, reviewed the clip-curation outputs of the ClipCannon pipeline, and consented to the subject-specific artefacts being used for the research purposes reported in §6.2 and §6.3. The subject retains the right to request deletion of any derived artefact; that right does not sunset at publication. No secondary human subjects appear in either case study, and no human-subjects review was triggered under the author's jurisdictional rules for single-subject self-research with documented consent. Case 1 uses the Shakespeare public-domain canon; no human subjects are involved.

**Asymmetric release posture.** To balance reproducibility against dual-use risk, the three cases adopt an asymmetric release posture. *Case 1* is scheduled for byte-identical release: the Shakespeare stylistic LoRA training stack, the DDA-projected training corpus derived from the public-domain canon, the three-layer enforcement stack, the 15/15 FSV battery, and pinned commit hashes will be released so that independent parties can reproduce the pipeline exactly. *Case 2* is released at the protocol level: the TCT protocol, the mouth-weighted flow-matching specification, the six forensic fixes with commit-level pointers, and the constellation construction procedure are disclosed, but the subject-specific training clips, the trained LoRA weights, and the compiled constellation are withheld because they would enable identity-locked video synthesis of a specific individual without additional consent gating. *Case 3* is released at the protocol level on the same grounds: the pipeline version hash, the best-of-12 selection protocol, the 50-clip centroid construction, the WavLM scorer configuration, and the threshold calibration procedure are disclosed, but the subject reference clips, the compiled speaker centroid, and the generated audio are withheld because they would enable identity-locked speech synthesis of a specific individual.

**Dual-use considerations.** Identity-locked audio-visual generation is a dual-use technology. The framework specified in §5 can be used for assistive technology (for example, voice restoration for speakers who have lost phonation) and for impersonation. The asymmetric release posture is the author's chosen balance between reproducibility obligation and identity-impersonation risk. Readers intending to deploy the protocol on a third-party identity should obtain explicit written consent covering the specific generative use.

**Data minimisation.** The training material for Cases 2 and 3 was drawn from a 16-minute single-subject interview consented for research use; no ambient personally identifiable information beyond the subject's own identity markers was processed. The constellation artefacts persist only the L2-normalised mean projections under the frozen embedder panel; raw audio and video are retained only for provenance audit and are not redistributed.

**Forensic disclosure.** Two forensic findings — the Shakespeare training-data echo-bug (§6.1.3) and the Santa flow-matching sign-bug (§6.2.2) — are disclosed so that independent researchers can avoid the same failure modes. We report them as engineering findings from the case record rather than as randomised ablations.
