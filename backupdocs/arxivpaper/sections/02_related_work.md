# 2. Related Work

This section situates the manuscript against six sub-fields of the 88-citation locked master pool, organised thematically rather than chronologically. The sub-fields correspond to clusters in our literature map: scaling laws and model collapse (Clusters A and B), synthetic-data mitigations (Cluster B continuation), compression (Cluster C), multi-embedder retrieval and evaluation (Cluster D), identity-locked zero-shot generation (Clusters E and F), and scalar-reward alignment (Cluster I). Auxiliary pipeline components (Cluster G) and enterprise / licensing signals (Cluster H) appear as context for §1 and are not developed here. The framing is pool-relative: every "no cited method" claim refers to the locked master pool, not the global literature. The DDA counting identity, collapse-silence scope argument, meaning-compression fourth-category proposal, TCT three-phase method, and constellation-guard predicate are §3 and §5 concerns deliberately not developed here.

## 2.1 The data wall and recursive collapse

Two independent measurement programmes jointly frame the contemporary data-supply problem. On the stock side, Villalobos et al. (2022/2024) estimate effective high-quality public human text at the order of 300 trillion tokens and project exhaustion between 2026 and 2032 under the compute-optimal scaling trajectories of Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022); overtraining regimes (for instance, Llama 3's roughly 200 tokens per parameter) accelerate that timeline by up to a factor of five. On the accessibility side, Longpre et al. (2024) audit the C4 crawl and report that roughly one quarter of its high-quality tokens became restricted by `robots.txt` or terms-of-service changes in a single twelve-month window, so that the accessible fraction of the corpus contracts faster than the total stock. The two studies use methodologically distinct instrumentation — demographic extrapolation of language-of-origin token counts versus crawler-accessibility auditing on a fixed corpus — and together constitute moderate evidence for the framing. A cluster of late-2024 industry reports (Woo & Efrati, 2024; Metz et al., 2024; Hu & Tong, 2024; Zeff, 2024; Sutskever, 2024) describes diminishing returns from raw-token pre-training; these cross-reference each other and are best read as one dated narrative signal rather than five independent confirmations. This manuscript takes the data wall as a peer-reviewed stock-and-accessibility claim accompanied by, not established by, the industry narrative.

Recursive self-training on generator-produced samples is the cleanest candidate mitigation to have been closed off theoretically. Shumailov et al. (2024), in *Nature*, derive and simulate an irreversible distributional drift under a specific recursion regime: each training round $t+1$ draws at least some samples from the prior generator's distribution, $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde{x} \sim p_{\theta_t}$, and the resulting fixed point loses distribution tails first ("early collapse") and then contracts variance toward the mode ("late collapse"). Dohmatob, Feng, Yang, Charton and Kempe (2024) re-express the drift as a scaling-law crossover in which the synthetic-mixture fraction shifts the exponents of the loss power-law; Alemohammad et al. (2023) document the same pattern empirically in image generators under the "MAD" (Model Autophagy Disorder) label. Across Gaussian mixtures, variational autoencoders, and language models, the cited results agree methodologically and constitute one of the better-established negative results in post-2022 generative modelling. The precondition is emphatically structural: every theorem and simulation in this sub-field requires a generator whose own outputs are subsequently fed back as training data. That precondition reappears in §3.3 as the pivot for this manuscript's scope claim — that a fixed real corpus projected through frozen, independently-trained embedders does not instantiate a generator-in-loop recursion and is therefore structurally not subject to, rather than a refutation of, the Shumailov regime. This manuscript does not contest the collapse theorems; it positions its contribution outside their scope.

## 2.2 Synthetic-data mitigations and their limits

Within the recursion regime, the cited literature proposes two mitigation families. **Accumulation** (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024; Alemohammad et al., 2023) keeps real data in the training mixture across generations rather than substituting synthetic for real, bounding test-error degradation at the cost of retaining the original corpus. **Verification** (Feng, Dohmatob, Yang, Charton, & Kempe, 2024; implicit in RLHF-style rejection) filters synthetic samples through an oracle or learned reward model before training, restoring scaling when rejection is sufficiently accurate. Seddik, Chen, Hayou, Youssef and Debbah (2024) statistically characterise both regimes. What unites the two families, and what the §2.1 scope claim turns on, is that both still operate inside the recursion loop: the unit of analysis is a sample drawn from a generator whose weights were trained on a prior round's data. Neither family has a theoretical place for the configuration in which a fixed corpus $D$ is held constant and $N$ frozen, independently-trained embedders compute deterministic projections $\phi_m(x)$ on each $x \in D$. The signals produced in that configuration are not samples from a generator; they are measurements taken by a frozen instrument on unchanged inputs. The accumulation and verification theorems are silent on this configuration, and the §3.3 scope argument builds on that silence rather than on any empirical counter-result.

## 2.3 Quantization and compression literature

A Shannon-anchored duality links next-token prediction to arithmetic coding, and recent work has made it operational. Delétang et al. (2024) run pre-trained language models as general-purpose compressors across text, image, and audio and report bits-per-byte competitive with PNG and FLAC — a Chinchilla-70B backbone compresses ImageNet to roughly 43% and LibriSpeech to roughly 16% of raw size. Valmeekam, Narayanan, Kalathil, Chamberland, and Shakkottai (2023) implement arithmetic coding directly on language-model logits ("LLMZip"), and Jiang et al. (2023) run the inverse move — a `gzip`-plus-$k$-nearest-neighbour pipeline beats several BERT baselines on low-resource text classification, suggesting that compression and learning sit closer than is usually acknowledged. Li et al. (2025) extend the lossless result into video. Huang et al. (2024) report a roughly linear correlation ($r \approx 0.95$) between compression ratio and downstream capability across 30+ LLMs, which is a large-$N$ observational claim within a single study and is cited as such. Adjacent to the compression-as-intelligence line, the standard taxonomy distinguishes three categories: **bit compression** (arithmetic coding, gzip, FLAC), **weight compression** (post-training quantisation such as GPTQ, one-shot pruning such as SparseGPT, and knowledge distillation), and **activation compression** (TurboQuant; KV-cache quantisation). All three measure economy of output relative to an input of the same modality: bits-per-bit, accuracy-per-parameter, memory-per-output-quality. None is a per-input labelled-signal-yield measurement, and none of the compression-as-intelligence papers prescribes compression *of the training corpus* via multiple frozen embedders. The §3.1 / §5.2 proposal that meaning compression is a fourth taxonomic entry with a semantically distinct unit (structured signals per raw-data unit) is a reading of, not a contradiction of, this cluster.

## 2.4 Multi-embedder retrieval and evaluation

The embedders this manuscript composes are drawn from a well-developed retrieval and representation literature. Dense vision-language embedders (SigLIP, Zhai, Mustafa, Kolesnikov, & Beyer, 2023; successors of the CLIP family), open text embedders (Nomic Embed, Nussbaum, Morris, Duderstadt, & Mulyar, 2024), speech self-supervised representations (WavLM, Chen et al., 2022), late-interaction retrieval (ColBERT, Khattab & Zaharia, 2020; ColBERTv2, Santhanam, Khattab, Saad-Falcon, Potts, & Zaharia, 2022), sparse lexical expansion (SPLADE, Formal, Piwowarski, & Clinchant, 2021), and knowledge-graph / entity embedders (KEPLER, Wang et al., 2021; TransE, Bordes, Usunier, Garcia-Durán, Weston, & Yakhnenko, 2013) together span architecturally distinct training objectives, corpora, and inductive biases. Approximate-nearest-neighbour infrastructure (HNSW, Malkov & Yashunin, 2018) makes retrieval over these projections tractable at scale, and reciprocal-rank fusion (RRF, Cormack, Clarke, & Büttcher, 2009) composes multiple rankings into a single ordering empirically robust to Condorcet and rank-learning baselines. Visual self-supervised embedders (DINOv2 and related backbones) are adjacent but outside the present pool; the argument here holds on the pool alone.

Across this cluster the embedders are evaluated as **retrieval instruments**: NDCG, Recall@$k$, equal-error-rate, mean average precision. Their outputs are the *endpoint* of the pipeline, and the composition of $N$ embedders is studied as a ranking-lift problem. No paper in the locked pool uses the same $N$ embedders as (i) a **labeller** producing structured supervisory signals on a fixed raw corpus at training-data-preparation time, and simultaneously (ii) a **runtime guard** applying a per-output cosine threshold against a frozen centroid set at inference. The retrieval literature is an enabling substrate — the signal cardinality $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ in §3.4 is a natural counting extension of multi-embedder composition — but treating $N$ embedders as $N$ independent views is established as a *retrieval* pattern, not a *labelling-and-training-signal* pattern. The role-flip (retriever to labeller to guard, over identical frozen instances, without parameter updates) is absent from the pool at the level of joint specification.

## 2.5 Identity-locked generation and alignment

Two threads are load-bearing: scalar-reward alignment as the dominant post-training paradigm, and identity-locked zero-shot generation as the application surface for Cases 2 and 3. Scalar-reward alignment is anchored by InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022), which learns a reward model $r_\psi$ on human preference pairs and maximises $\mathbb{E}[r_\psi(y \mid x)]$ under a KL-to-reference regulariser; Direct Preference Optimisation (Rafailov, Sharma, Mitchell, Ermon, Manning, & Finn, 2023) re-expresses the RLHF objective as a closed-form loss on preference pairs with implicit reward $r(y|x) = \beta \log(\pi_\theta / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$; Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) substitutes an AI critique against natural-language rules for human preferences while retaining the reward-model / preference-optimisation structure. Citing these three is a deliberate scope device, not a survey claim: RLAIF, KTO, IPO, ORPO, and the broader reward-modelling landscape are deferred to §9 future work. In all three anchors the optimisation primitive is a scalar, and the runtime behaviour of the aligned model is uninstrumented: no per-output geometric check, no multi-modal conjunction at inference.

Zero-shot voice cloning is conventionally framed as in-context learning from a single speaker prompt. VALL-E (Wang et al., 2023) introduced the neural-codec language-model formulation; VALL-E 2 (Chen et al., 2024) declared human parity on LibriSpeech continuation via SIM / WER / DNSMOS parity with a reported WavLM-large SV cosine around 0.881; successor systems — NaturalSpeech 3 (Ju et al., 2024), MaskGCT (Wang et al., 2024), F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2024), Voicebox (Le et al., 2023), NaturalSpeech 2 (Shen et al., 2023), Mega-TTS 2 (Jiang et al., 2023), XTTS (Casanova, Davis, Gölge, et al., 2024) — cluster in the 0.86–0.89 WavLM-cosine band under single-prompt conditioning on LibriSpeech-derivative corpora. Evaluation vocabulary is shared: DNSMOS P.808/P.835 (Reddy, Gopal, & Cutler, 2022) for naturalness, UTMOS (Saeki et al., 2022) for neural MOS, ECAPA-TDNN (Desplanques, Thienpondt, & Demuynck, 2020) and WavLM (Chen et al., 2022) for speaker verification, and the open CloneEval benchmark (Christop, Kuczyński, & Kubis, 2025). Two caveats travel with the frontier framing. First, VALL-E 2 and NaturalSpeech 3 are closed-artefact paradigm anchors — no public weights, training data, or evaluation code — so their headline numbers cannot be independently reproduced and are cited as paradigm references rather than pool-comparable benchmarks. Second, all cited systems use single-prompt conditioning; none aggregates a multi-clip reference set into an L2-normalised per-modality centroid held frozen through training and inference, and none applies a per-output cosine gate against such a centroid at inference.

Identity-locked talking-head synthesis is an active and rapidly-moving sub-field. EchoMimicV3 (Meng et al., 2025) — the base model for Case 2 — is a 1.3B-parameter flow-matching diffusion-transformer with target convention $\text{target} = \text{noise} - \text{latent}$; Hallo3 (Cui et al., 2024), LatentSync (Li et al., 2024), HunyuanVideo-Avatar (Chen et al., 2025), and EMO / EMO2 (Tian, Wang, Zhang, & Bo, 2024; Tian, Hu, Wang, Zhang, & Bo, 2025) occupy adjacent design space, with text-to-video foundation models (CogVideoX, Yang et al., 2024; Wan, Wan Team, 2025) supplying the broader lineage. Subject-specific adaptation is typically handled by low-rank adaptation (LoRA, Hu et al., 2022) on top of a flow-matching loss (Lipman, Chen, Ben-Hamu, Nickel, & Le, 2023), conditioned on a reference image plus audio; structural identity is grounded in FLAME (Li, Bolkart, Black, Li, & Romero, 2017) and the FACS action-unit taxonomy (Ekman & Friesen, 1978). Evaluation uses LSE-D, LSE-C, FVD, and CSIM post-generation on held-out sets. As in the TTS sub-field and in §2.4, no cited system constructs a multi-modal frozen centroid set across visual, speaker, voice, emotion, and expression modalities, and none applies a runtime per-modality cosine gate at inference. Case 2's forensic disclosure — an under-documented flow-matching sign-convention hazard in the EchoMimicV3 lineage, diagnosed during subject-specific LoRA training — is the kind of engineering finding the sub-field's arXiv-only papers omit; §6 reports it as engineering narrative rather than randomised ablation.

Across voice cloning, talking-head synthesis, and scalar-reward alignment, three absences are structural: no frozen multi-modal geometric target, no per-output runtime acceptance predicate, and no multi-modal conjunction over independent embedders. Those are what §5 picks up under "constellation set", "constellation guard", and "per-modality conjunction". The comparison with scalar-reward alignment is pool-relative and not a benchmark contest: this manuscript does not claim TCT outperforms InstructGPT, DPO, or Constitutional AI on preference or win-rate benchmarks, and makes no assertion about preference-alignment quality. The narrower claim §5 defends is that cosine proximity to a frozen multi-modal centroid set admits per-output verifiability in a technical sense scalar-reward methods do not, given the cited pool.

## 2.6 Gaps this paper addresses

Six gaps are load-bearing for the remainder of the manuscript and are surfaced here rather than re-derived later. (i) **Signals-per-input is unformalised**: no cited paper measures or prescribes labelled-signal density per raw-data unit; the data-wall literature counts raw tokens, compression-as-intelligence reads compression from trained models, and retrieval treats embedders as rankers. (ii) **No third path is named** on the data-wall menu: the cited mitigations are acquire-more and synthesise-more, and decomposition of a fixed corpus through $N$ frozen embedders is absent as a named category. (iii) **No per-output runtime geometric guard** exists across retrieval, TTS, identity-locked video, and alignment: all evaluate similarity after generation on held-out sets, and none runs a per-output cosine-to-centroid threshold during generation. (iv) **Multi-clip centroid aggregation is not the convention**: zero-shot TTS uses single-prompt in-context learning and video systems condition on reference images, and no cited system aggregates a 50-clip reference set into an L2-normalised per-modality centroid held frozen as an alignment target. (v) **Formal embedder-independence measurement is missing**: the $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise-interaction count §3.4 will state is an upper bound under an approximate-independence axiom, and no mutual-information protocol in the pool measures its tightness on a fixed corpus projected through an $N$-embedder panel. (vi) **No forensic register** for flow-matching sign-convention or training-data echo-bug hazards: none of the cited generative-modelling papers publishes the kind of pipeline-forensic disclosure Cases 1 and 2 will report.

These gaps are anchored in the locked 88-citation master pool and are not global-literature claims. Section 3 addresses (i), (ii), (iii) structurally and (v) as an axiomatic hedge; §5 addresses (iii) and (iv) by method; §6 reports one measured and two projected instantiations; §6's forensic paragraphs address (vi). Three further items — the collapse-silence scope argument, the TCT / scalar-reward non-equivalence, and the guard's scaling to many modalities and subjects — are staged for §3, §5, and §7 respectively. Two further items — the scaling-law re-fit under DDA-enriched supervision and the multi-speaker same-session WavLM band characterisation — are routed to §9 future work, consistent with the split between the constructive sample-complexity theorem of §5 and its single-operating-point empirical anchor in Case 3. On this foundation, §3 now formalises DDA and meaning compression and §5 specifies the TCT three-phase method.
