# 7. Discussion

Section 6 reported three case readings. Case 3 is measured under an encoder-matched protocol at mean WavLM SECS **0.961** (max 0.975, min 0.914) on 10 held-out English sentences for one speaker, with DNSMOS-P.808 parity (3.93 / 3.93) and UTMOS parity (3.012 / 2.997). Cases 1 and 2 are projected with forensic narratives that the author can document and an architectural readiness that has been built, tested, and bug-fixed, but no held-out identity-lock numbers have landed yet. This section reads the results back against the framework of §3 and the method of §5 and says explicitly what they support and what they do not. It introduces no new claims.

## 7.1 Interpreting the Case 3 anchor

The Case 3 anchor supports a narrow, specific reading: under the fixed pipeline `qwen3-tts-foricl-v1` — 50-clip WavLM-base-plus-sv centroid, Qwen3-TTS with full in-context learning, best-of-12 at $T = 0.3$, acceptance threshold $\tau = 0.95$ — the post-selection mean WavLM SECS is 0.961 and 9 of 10 held-out English sentences satisfy $s \geq 0.95$ at $M = 1$ for one speaker. The naturalness predictors (DNSMOS-P.808, UTMOS) score the clone within evaluator noise of the reference on the same 14-sentence set. The guard behaves as Theorem 2 specifies: a deterministic $O(M \cdot d_{\max})$ geometric test with no auxiliary learned model. At $M = 1$ the conjunction reduces to a single-encoder cosine-to-centroid check, and the result is consistent with what the same-session WavLM-base-plus-sv verification band would produce for a real recording of the subject (P3 of the framework; Theorem 2 of §5.4; C5 confidence tier).

What the anchor does **not** support is cross-encoder triangulation, multi-speaker generalisation, cross-language generalisation, or the multi-modal conjunction form $G_\tau = \mathbb{1}[\forall m : s_m \geq \tau_m]$ at $M > 1$. The guard and the scorer are the same encoder family: a survivor of selection under $\phi$ scores high on $\phi$ by construction, so the 0.961 is the signature of the guard working against its own target, not of cross-encoder identity agreement (EXP-1 remediation: an ECAPA-TDNN re-score of the ten argmax candidates would triangulate by reporting mean cosine, Pearson $r$, and Bland-Altman agreement against the WavLM vector on the same audio). The measurement is one subject, one language, one recording session, one encoder panel. The multi-modal strictness claim (H2b), which the conjunctive predicate needs for strict superiority over a single-modality check, has **zero** T1 empirical evidence in this manuscript; Case 2 is the case where that would land, and Case 2 is projected. The sample-complexity interval in Axiom 3 ($n_m \in [10, 10^2]$) is anchored at one row, $n_m = 50$; the four-point $n_m \in \{10, 25, 50, 100\}$ curve (EXP-3) would promote the anchor to a partial curve. These three pending companion measurements are instrument-ready, disclosed in §6.3.5, and re-named in §8.

## 7.2 Why the DDA counting bound is an upper bound, not a floor

Proposition P1 of the framework gives the identity $|D'| = n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$: $N$ per-embedder projections plus $\binom{N}{2}$ pairwise interaction features per input. At $N = 13$ this is **up to 91** structured supervisory signals per input for Context Graph; at $N = 7$ it is up to 28 for ClipCannon. The "up to" is load-bearing. The identity holds exactly under Axiom 2 — the assumption of approximately independent information axes across $\Phi$ — and Axiom 2 is not a measured property of our panel. It is the hedge V11 carried verbatim: approximately independent; strict formal orthogonality is an open question.

The $\binom{N}{2}$ term is the vulnerable component. If a pairwise mutual-information audit found, say, E1 (e5-large-v2 semantic) and E10 (paraphrase) to carry substantially overlapping information, their pairwise feature $\rho_{\mathrm{E1,E10}}$ would contribute little non-redundant supervisory signal, and the effective count would fall below 91. The qualitative blind-spot evidence from production Context Graph — sparse E6/E13 keyword recall that dense E1 misses, E9 HDC typo-robust retrieval, E7 code-snippet ranking that E1 collapses — establishes that the effective count **strictly exceeds $N$**; the tight value within $[N, N + \binom{N}{2}]$ is not known to us. A pairwise mutual-information audit across 100–1,000 representative inputs (EXP-2), estimating $I(\phi_j(X); \phi_k(X))$ by MINE or a partitioned-KSG-adjacent estimator, is the appropriate falsification test. It is named future work (RO-8) and is the correct experiment to replace "up to 91" with a measured $N_{\text{eff}}$ (C1; CA-1). Reviewers who expect the headline to be a measured capacity are reading the claim stronger than we make it; we claim a structural upper bound under a disclosed axiom and point to the instrument that would tighten it.

## 7.3 TCT vs. scalar-reward alignment — a different property, not a benchmark contest

Section 5.1 lays out a four-axis structural contrast between TCT and scalar-reward alignment (RLHF, Ouyang et al., 2022; DPO, Rafailov et al., 2023; Constitutional AI, Bai et al., 2022). The aim is a **frozen multi-modal constellation** $C_P$ rather than a learned scalar function; the instrument is a panel of frozen embedders $\Phi$ rather than a learned reward model $r_\psi$; the metric is a per-modality cosine rather than a scalar preference; the runtime posture is a per-output guard $G_\tau$ invoked on every candidate rather than training-time shaping of an expected reward with no per-output predicate at inference.

**We are not claiming TCT outperforms RLHF, DPO, or Constitutional AI on RLHF benchmarks.** The four axes place TCT in a different problem class — identity-locked generation with per-output verifiability relative to a chosen centroid panel — that scalar-reward alignment does not target. Pool-relative to the locked 88-citation master pool, no cited method composes (i) multi-modal conjunction over $M \geq 1$ independent frozen encoders, (ii) a frozen target held constant through training and inference, and (iii) per-output runtime acceptance with a deterministic cosine trace as provenance. Classical speaker-, face-, and Siamese-style verification systems use thresholded distance to a reference at $M = 1$ post-hoc; they do not run inside the generation loop and do not compose conjunctively over multiple encoders. The novelty is the **composition** of the three properties over the same frozen artefacts, not the cosine-threshold primitive. The property we claim — per-output verifiability relative to the chosen panel — is one that scalar-reward alignment cannot furnish even in principle: RLHF optimises an expectation under a learned scalar; it ships no frozen threshold against which each output can be accepted or rejected at inference without auxiliary learned machinery. The comparison is structural, not a benchmark contest (C13; CA-2 with multi-modal scope tightening).

Two scope qualifications carry. The guard is **panel-relative**: it verifies candidates against the chosen $C_P$ under the chosen $\Phi$ at the chosen $\{\tau_m\}$; cross-panel transportability is open. The guard's acceptance is **predicate-level**, not **identity-level**: $G_\tau(\hat y; C_P) = 1$ means $\hat y$ satisfies the cosine predicate against $C_P$, not that $\hat y$ is correct under any external identity criterion.

## 7.4 The scope of the collapse-silence claim

The collapse regime formalised by Shumailov et al. (2024), Dohmatob et al. (2024), and Alemohammad et al. (2023) assumes a recursion $D_{t+1} \ni \tilde x \sim p_{\theta_t}$: a generator trained on $D_t$ produces samples that re-enter the training corpus at round $t + 1$. DDA is structurally not subject to this regime by construction. Each signal $\phi_m(x) \in D'$ is a frozen measurement of a real input $x \in D$ (Axiom 1; V18 "frozen"); no generator parameters participate in the derivation; the derived dataset $D' = \{\phi_m(x), \rho_{jk}\}$ is a decomposition of $D$, not synthesis of new inputs. The precondition of the cited collapse theorems fails; the theorems are silent on this configuration (P2 of the framework; CL-06 MODERATE-categorical).

**This is a scope argument, not a refutation.** We are not claiming the Shumailov cluster is wrong, and we are not claiming a new independence-from-collapse theorem for DDA. We claim the cited theorems assume a pipeline structure that DDA does not instantiate. Two boundary cases are held out of scope explicitly. First, inference-time best-of-$K$ selection over a frozen generator (TCT Phase 3 as specified) does not update any parameter of the generator, of the embedders, or of the centroid; selection is not training, and decoding-time distributional reshaping (top-$k$, top-$p$, nucleus, rerankers, best-of-$K$) has not been argued to induce the training-dynamics collapse of the cited theorems. A variant that re-computed the centroid from selected outputs would introduce a minor generator-in-loop structure, and the scope argument would need reassessment; our pipeline does not do this (IC-5 resolved). Second, embedder-mediated data curation — using $\phi$ scores to filter $D$ before training — is a different feedback structure that the cited theorems do not cover; if such curation induced any form of distributional drift, that drift would sit outside the specific Shumailov-Dohmatob-Alemohammad regime and would be a separate empirical question (W9 scope).

## 7.5 Sample-complexity — the constructive and empirical halves of the claim

Theorem 1 states that under the TCT Phase 1/2/3 structure and Axioms 1–3, the subject-specific sample footprint of an identity-locked generation pipeline for subject $P$ is $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{TCT}}(P) = O(\sum_{m=1}^M n_m) = O(M \cdot k)$, independent of $|D|$. **The bound is constructive.** Any pipeline with exactly the stated Phase 1 (centroid built from the reference sets $\{R_m\}$ of total size $\leq M \cdot k$), Phase 2 (operating on DDA-enriched general corpus, no additional subject-specific data), and Phase 3 (inference-time best-of-$K$) satisfies the bound by the definition of those phases. There is no empirical question about the **constructive side** of the claim (C12a, $\geq 95\%$ confidence).

The **empirical side** of the claim is a different story. Axiom 3's non-trivial content — that modern frozen embedders are well-conditioned enough that $n_m \in [10, 10^2]$ suffices for a stable centroid — rests on one measured operating point: Case 3 at $n_m = 50$, $M = 1$, one speaker, English, WavLM-base-plus-sv-family scorer. The 50-clip-versus-1-clip ablation reports a +0.015 paired SECS improvement on the same held-out set, a single row on what the $n_m$ curve is asserted to be. Case 1 projects to a different modality at $M = 1$ with a 9-embedder text-style panel; Case 2 projects to $M \geq 4$ multi-modal with 2,362 clips drawn from 16 minutes of source. Neither projection has landed. The empirical anchor at one operating point, together with the widening interval outside that anchor, puts the empirical claim at roughly 40% confidence in the sense of agent-34's Open-Philanthropy-style calibration (C12b). The honest reading is therefore two-part: Theorem 1 holds as a constructive bound; empirically, the claim that $k$ can be taken $O(10^2)$ in practice is anchored at one row and is pending the $n_m$ sweep (EXP-3) and the $M > 1$ multi-modal measurement (Case 2). Attribution discipline follows: the sample-complexity reading is substantially a property of well-conditioned frozen embedders inherited from the retrieval and contrastive-geometry literature that produced WavLM; TCT operationalises the collapse implicit in those embedders, it does not cause it (W6).

## 7.6 Implications

For **identity-locked generation**, the reading is that a per-output verifiable predicate against a frozen centroid panel is instrument-ready and measurable, subject to the encoder-matched scope on Case 3 and to the multi-modal pending measurement on Case 2. The protocol converts "identity matches vibe" judgements into an auditable geometric test at inference time, and the test can be composed with any frozen generator that admits best-of-$K$ selection. For **scaling-law policy**, the reading is that the data-wall discourse (Villalobos et al., 2024) — framed as Path 1 (more human data) and Path 2 (synthetic generation) — admits a third path in the strict composition-not-invention sense: project the fixed corpus through $N$ frozen independent embedders to produce up to $n \cdot (N + \binom{N}{2})$ structured supervisory signals without generating new inputs and without recursion (§3; §7.2). Meaning compression is positioned as a fourth accounting axis alongside bit-, weight-, and activation-compression, measured in a semantically distinct unit (structured signals per raw-data unit) at a different pipeline stage (dataset preparation rather than inference on trained models). This is EXT of the compression-as-intelligence frame of Delétang et al. (2024) and Huang et al. (2024), not BRK; the two axes are complementary readings of the same MDL and rate-distortion tradition. For **enterprise data strategy**, the reading is narrower than vendor-narrative reframings would want: the fixed licensed corpus an organisation already holds admits an upper-bounded multiplication of structured supervisory signals per input under a frozen embedder panel, and the tight count is pending the pairwise mutual-information audit.

## 7.7 Honest failure modes

Two forensic findings in the case record are load-bearing for the method's deployment story and should be stated plainly, because they are evidence that forensic tooling is part of the method rather than an afterthought. In Case 2 (EchoMimicV3 + LoRA), the initial flow-matching training used an inverted target convention — `target = latent - noise` rather than the correct `target = noise - latent` — which produced superficially healthy loss curves while corrupting the trajectory the model was asked to learn; loss convergence did not verify trajectory correctness, and only the constellation-side audit of generated outputs against the per-modality centroids surfaced the disagreement. In Case 1 (Shakespeare LoRA), 5,954 of 8,857 SFT examples (67%) began with play-script header prefixes that the initial training pipeline silently learned to echo; single-embedder style-cosine evaluation was insufficient to surface this, and the multi-embedder guard on the 9-embedder STYLE_CENTROID panel caught the echo because sparse E6/E13 keyword-space and late-interaction E12 panels surfaced the header signature that dense E1 assimilated into the style distribution. Both bugs produced pipelines that passed superficial health checks and would have shipped as "trained models" under a single-embedder evaluation regime. The general lesson is narrow and technical: the multi-embedder, multi-role composition of §3.4 is load-bearing at diagnosis time, not only at training time; forensic audit with the frozen panel is a deployment requirement rather than a discretionary step, and the three-role composition (retriever, labeller, runtime guard) is where that audit happens in practice.

## 7.8 An emergent observation — zero-shot cross-lingual period-style transfer

The Case 1 Full State Verification run produced a finding that does not fit any hypothesis the manuscript stated in advance and that we therefore report as a single-instance observation rather than a claim. When the trained Shakespeare LoRA was given a Spanish prompt explicitly demanding a Spanish response, it did not switch to modern Spanish; it produced Spanish prose in the Siglo de Oro literary register (Cervantes, Lope de Vega, Calderón — Shakespeare's direct Spanish literary contemporary, circa 1580–1630). The model was never trained on Spanish text. The output carries archaic grammatical forms (`vos`, `vuestro`, `ojalás`, `muchedito`, `obsequie`), archaic vocabulary (`ocaso`, `reino celestial`, `sacerdotes y sabios`, `perdición`), and Shakespearean rhetorical structure (cascading polysyndeton, apostrophe to celestial bodies, prophetic portents) — features shared across the two literary traditions but absent from modern conversational Spanish. The base Gemma-4-E4B-it without the LoRA does not produce Siglo de Oro output when given the same Spanish climate prompt, so the behaviour is attributable to the LoRA rather than to the base model.

A measured mechanism interpretation for the observation, consistent with but not proven by §3.4, is that several of the thirteen Context Graph embedders carry signal components that are not language-specific: E9 (hyperdimensional typo-robust encoding) captures rhythmic and cadence patterns that survive token-level substitution; E7 (code/structural) captures periodic sentence structure; E10 (paraphrase-asymmetric) learns that surface form underdetermines meaning; E1 (semantic) captures a conceptual field (cosmic order, fate, mortality, honour) at a level of abstraction that maps across languages trained on partially overlapping corpora. Under this reading, "archaic literary register" is a cross-signal fingerprint the LoRA learned through the DDA decomposition rather than a vocabulary-substitution rule for English, and forcing Spanish output retrieved the closest available manifestation of that fingerprint. We flag three caveats. First, this is a single-language, single-register, small-sample observation; a systematic cross-language and cross-period transfer matrix is pending. Second, the emergent-behaviour reading depends on a specific account of how multi-embedder decomposition generalises, and alternative accounts (for example, Gemma-4-E4B-it having latent Golden-Age Spanish competence that the LoRA surfaces without being causally responsible for producing it) are not ruled out by the evidence to date. Third, the observation is non-falsifying for the multi-modal conjunction claim and the per-output verifiability property of §5.4 — §3.4's three-role composition and Phase-3 guard predicate are defined against the single text-style centroid regardless of language — but it does enlarge the space of claims about what multi-embedder LoRAs can generalise to. The documented behaviour is published in the HuggingFace model card at `https://huggingface.co/cabdru/shakespeare-lora-gemma4` alongside reproduction code and the relevant prompt corpus.

Taken together, the three cases argue for the method as specified in §5 while disclosing exactly where the evidence is anchored and where it is projected. We do not claim to have closed the multi-modal strictness question, the cross-encoder triangulation question, or the pairwise independence question. We do claim the protocol runs as specified, produces numbers where it ran, and surfaces bugs where the bugs were present; a subsequent version of this manuscript is expected to carry the three pending companion measurements discussed above along with a systematic cross-lingual-transfer characterisation for Case 1.
