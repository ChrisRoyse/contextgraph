## M — Methodological limitations

1. **Six themes vs. four themes trade-off.** Agent-17 kept six themes
   rather than collapsing C + D or E + F for narrative compactness.
   The six-theme choice preserves section-level specificity (§2.5 vs.
   §2.6; §3.6 vs. §6.3 spines). A four-theme version would yield a
   tighter abstract but would conflate the scalar-vs-geometric and
   single-vs-multi-encoder arguments that the manuscript benefits from
   keeping separate. Agent-42 may request the tighter set for abstract
   purposes; agent-35 (abstract-writer) is the natural owner.
2. **Out-of-pool paradigm borrow.** Theme E's runtime-verification
   frame is borrowed from formal methods, which is not represented
   in the 88-citation pool. Agent-17 treats this as a paradigm anchor
   (not a citation) and notes it explicitly in the handoff. Agent-18
   and agent-42 will probe whether in-pool proxies exist.
3. **Convergence-point fragility.** The three narrative convergence
   points (C+D→E, A+B→F, E→§6.3) require downstream section-writers
   to maintain thematic discipline. If §5.3 drops the geometric-
   alignment frame or the multi-encoder-conjunction frame, the
   convergence breaks. Agents 37 and 38 must read the narrative-arc
   file before writing §5.
4. **MP-13 foundational-SS infrastructure role.** Three canonical
   citations (`hu2022lora`, `lipman2023flowmatching`,
   `malkov2018hnsw`) carry disproportionate weight across Themes B
   and F. Agent-17 documents this as citation-economics, not
   evidential deficiency, but agent-40 (limitations-writer) must
   carry the honest-reporting paragraph.
5. **Theme F asymmetry.** Only Case 3 is measured; Cases 1 and 2 are
   T3-projected. The theme's empirical weight rests on a single
   author-empirical anchor (CL-16). Agent-17 acknowledges this and
   routes the projected-vs-measured ratio to agent-18 + agent-40 for
   §8 Limitations.
