## Source 1 — Forbes / Maha Abouelenein (2024-09-27) — PRIMARY ANCHOR

**URL**: https://www.forbes.com/sites/mahaabouelenein/2024/09/27/networking-like-a-pro-8-tips-for-networking-at-events/
**Author**: Maha Abouelenein, CEO of Digital & Savvy, Forbes contributor
**Recency**: 19 months old → current-era advice (post-AI, post-pandemic norms)

**The 8-Step Playbook (verbatim headings)**:
1. Share in advance that you are going to be attending — post on social, signal mutual connections
2. Study the event program and agenda — highlight sessions/speakers to attend + meet
3. Develop a list of key people to meet — follow on social, track recent activity
4. DM speakers or attendees — establish connections before the event
5. Meet the event organizers — connect with programming + sponsors staff
6. Have a QR code with your business card — quick exchange
7. Follow up fast — send a photo + your contact info immediately after meeting
8. Volunteer to speak to the press — free publicity + deliver key messages

**Governing principle (verbatim)**: "the true purpose of networking is to create value...
the secret to networking is that you need to focus on bringing value to others first."

**Anchor phrase for Chris/Steve**: "Don't just register and show up, especially if you
paid for travel and accommodations."

## Source 2 — Forbes Agency Council / Serial Conference-Goer (2016-08-10) — PROCESS FRAMEWORK

**URL**: https://www.forbes.com/sites/forbesagencycouncil/2016/08/10/inside-tips-from-a-serial-conference-goer/
**Author profile**: "Seasoned marketing technologist, 15+ conferences/year, 200+ conferences attended across industries"

**Pre-conference phase**:
- Sit with team, define who attends + what role each person plays
- Review agenda, identify must-see speakers
- Workshop selection: focus on business-gap areas + industry innovations
- Booth target list: companies with products/services useful to your customers
- Networking event shortlist
- **The "Thirds Rule"**: 1/3 sessions, 1/3 networking/meetings, 1/3 exhibitors
- **"Have your team spend several weeks making pre-conference calls, setting meetings,
  and making travel arrangements"** — this is the Steve warm-up principle, explicit

**On-site phase**:
- Check in, badge, download conference app (if exists)
- **Send confirmation texts** to every scheduled meeting that day
- Practice pitch before each meeting
- Familiarize with each new contact via LinkedIn + their company website
- "Conference arsenal": cards, coffee, water, mints, sanitizer, notepad, pen, portable
  charger, game face
- **12–15 solid meetings/day** is the serial-goer benchmark
- End-of-day: review notes, prioritize client potential, prep tomorrow

**Post-conference phase**:
- "Give yourself a day to decompress but don't disconnect completely"
- Post-conference debrief with team, decide first-contact priority
- Summary email + follow-up-call request + handwritten thank-you note
- Track opportunity versus pre-conference goals

## Source 3 — Forbes / Darrah Brustein (2018-04-01) — CONVERSATION OPENERS

**URL**: https://www.forbes.com/sites/darrahbrustein/2018/04/01/15-ways-to-start-a-conversation-at-a-conference-without-feeling-weird/

**The 15 openers relevant to Chris's introvert-technical-founder profile**:

1. Relax — everyone is there to connect (mindset)
2. Talk to the person beside you at a session — ask "Are you familiar with this speaker?"
   or "I'm looking forward to ___. What compelled you to choose this session?"
3. Post-session: approach someone who asked a great question ("I really loved that question
   you asked" = low-friction opener)
4. Ask a question during Q&A yourself — makes you visible; people approach after
5. Body language: open posture (not folded arms/looking away)
6. Hang out near food/drink areas — refreshment = natural pause for hellos
7. Even bathroom-line conversations are fair game
8. Sit in the lobby with badge visible — lower-key environment
9. **Do NOT scan everyone's badges looking for your target** — "creepy" marker
10. Get introduced by a wingperson ("have you met my friend?")
11. **Pre-book appointments via attendee list/app — "to collaborate (never to sell them
    something)"** — key framing for Chris's academic/technical approach
12. Use pre-planned networking windows (happy hours etc.)
13. "Post up" at a highboy table with coffee; nod + smile + eye contact when people pass
14. Be open to everyone (bartenders, chair-movers — you never know)
15. Be yourself — "doing that inauthentically from the start will do you, and them, no favors"

**Recurring principle across all three sources**: "connect, don't sell." Especially
important for Chris at Milken / FII — he is pitching a primitive, not a product. Brustein's
framing #11 ("collaborate, never sell") is the exact positioning.

## Derived guidance for Chris + Steve

Combining the three sources:

1. **Pre-event timeline is measured in WEEKS, not days** (Serial Goer: "have your team
   spend several weeks"). This means FII 10 (Oct 26) warm-up starts late August. Milken
   Global LA (May 3) is already inside the "1-week" window.
2. **Pre-event DM outreach is industry-standard**, not bold or unusual (Abouelenein #4).
   Chris should LinkedIn-DM / X-DM target attendees 3–8 weeks out with collaboration
   framing (never pitch).
3. **"Collaborate, never sell" framing** matches our primitive-first doctrine perfectly
   (Brustein #11). TCT is a primitive to be critiqued / absorbed, not a product to be
   closed.
4. **12–15 meetings/day is the professional-tier cadence** — Steve + Chris should plan
   ~10 pre-booked + 4–5 walk-up slots per conference day.
5. **Thirds Rule** gives clear time allocation: at a 3-day FII, that's ~1 day of sessions
   (to be seen + learn), ~1 day of meetings, ~1 day of exhibitors + floor networking.
6. **Relational, not transactional** (Roizen) — Chris's posture should be "I'm here to
   critique my primitive against people who build models/invest at scale," not "I'm here
   to raise money."
