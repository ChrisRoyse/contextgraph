# 9. Related Work

The framework sits at the intersection of four literatures: symbol grounding, information-theoretic representation, calibrated/abstaining prediction, and benchmark integrity. This section places it against each, noting what is borrowed and what is new.

## 9.1 Symbol grounding and the dictionary

Harnad's symbol grounding problem [Harnad1990] is the conceptual root: symbols defined only by symbols never reach meaning. Vincent-Lamarre et al. [VincentLamarre2016] made the structure quantitative — the grounding Kernel (≈10%) and the Minimal Grounding Set (≈1%, a minimum feedback vertex set, NP-hard per [Karp1972]) — and Steyvers & Tenenbaum [Steyvers2005] showed the mental lexicon is a small-world, scale-free association graph grown by differentiation. This paper's contribution is to read a *domain predictor* as exactly this object, with the oracle as the grounding channel, and to make the kernel a computable target for an arbitrary domain (§4). Mollo & Millière's *Vector Grounding Problem* [Mollo2023] updates Harnad for systems that compute over vectors and supplies the teleosemantic grounding criterion (causal-informational relation + selection history) that this paper turns into the sensor-admission test of §6.5. Where prior grounding work is largely diagnostic ("are these representations grounded?"), the contribution here is *operational*: grounding becomes a measurement (`I(panel; oracle)`) and a selection procedure (bit-contribution assembly).

## 9.2 Information-theoretic representation and estimation

The substrate-sufficiency test (§5) rests on classical information theory — the data-processing inequality and Fano's inequality [CoverThomas2006; Fano1961] — and on the information bottleneck view of representation [Tishby1999], in which a good representation preserves `I(·;Y)` while compressing `X`. The estimators are MINE [Belghazi2018], InfoNCE [vandenOord2018], the KSG k-NN estimator [Kraskov2004], and the unifying variational treatment of Poole et al. [Poole2019]. The novelty is not the estimators but their *use as a gate*: applying the data-processing ceiling as a method-independent existence test for a grounding kernel, run before model training, with per-cell and drop-one decompositions that diagnose *which modality* is missing. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis [Huh2024] supplies the theoretical license to read a heterogeneous sensor panel as coordinates against one structure — its result that converged representations approach the pointwise mutual information over latent generative events is the same PMI structure the kernel and the substrate test are built on.

## 9.3 World models, calibration, and abstaining prediction

The architecture is a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture [LeCun2022]: prediction in representation space, heterogeneous non-shared encoders, a non-generative predictor — which this paper extends with the slot-identity invariant and the constellation-as-definition operator (§6.3). Downstream, the predictor uses standard calibration — Brier score [Brier1950], expected calibration error and temperature/Platt scaling [Guo2017] — and conformal prediction [Angelopoulos2021] for distribution-free coverage with an explicit abstain. Continual update under mistake feedback uses elastic-weight-style protection against catastrophic forgetting [Kirkpatrick2017]. The contribution here is to subordinate all of this to the substrate test: §5.2 shows calibration and conformal coverage are bounded by `I(panel;oracle)` and therefore cannot rescue an insufficient panel — a point easy to miss when calibration is treated as the final quality lever.

## 9.4 Benchmark integrity and the oracle

The oracle-validity concern (§2.3, §7.6) draws directly on the SWE-bench [Jimenez2024] data-quality literature: Aleithan et al.'s SWE-Bench+ audit (solution leakage, weak tests, pre-cutoff contamination) [Aleithan2024], Wang et al.'s differential-testing study showing weak-test inflation [Wang2025], and OpenAI's audit and retirement of SWE-bench Verified for flawed tests and training-set leakage [OpenAI2026]. The contribution is to fold these findings into the predicate as *measurements* — flakiness via replica voting (Tier-5.2), validity and contamination as named open audits — rather than treating the oracle as ground truth by fiat. Goodhart's law and its variants [Manheim2018] motivate the cross-panel guard (Tier-1.3) and the rejection of proxy metrics like rank-lift (§7.5).

## 9.5 Measures of intelligence

Chollet's measure of intelligence [Chollet2019] defines intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency — skill from few examples, relative to priors. The constellation-as-definition operator (§6.3) is a concrete instance: a Pass/Fail region learned from `O(k)` grounded examples, with the kernel (§4) being the minimal grounded set such a learner needs. Where Chollet's ARC targets broad fluid intelligence, this paper targets the narrower, falsifiable notion of *operational super-intelligence in a domain with an oracle* (§1.4) — trading generality for a checkable predicate.

## 9.6 What is new here, in one paragraph

The borrowed pieces are the grounding result, the MI estimators, the JEPA stance, the calibration toolbox, and the benchmark-integrity findings. The new synthesis is: (i) the oracle elevated to first-class object with a flakiness/validity decomposition and a self-consistency ceiling; (ii) the domain predictor read as a grounded association engine with a computable grounding kernel; (iii) the substrate-sufficiency test as a method-independent, pre-training existence test for that kernel, with per-cell/per-sensor diagnosis and a bit-contribution sensor-assembly procedure; and (iv) a falsifiable super-intelligence predicate that ties all of these into one checkable condition — demonstrated on a live system that the framework correctly falsifies at the substrate gate.
