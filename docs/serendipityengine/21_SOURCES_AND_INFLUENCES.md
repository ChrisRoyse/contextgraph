## 6. What we did *not* draw from (and why)

- **Andrew Tate / red-pill / pickup-artist literature.** Not because they're contemptible (though they are) but because they offer no insight relevant to civic flourishing. Mentioned only to be explicit about exclusion.
- **Modern crypto / Web3 token-governance literature.** Token-weighted voting is plutocratic by construction; sortition is the correct mechanism (`14` §8).
- **Big-tech growth-hack literature (Andrew Chen, Brian Balfour, Reforge).** Optimizing growth is opposed to our optimizing objective (`16`).
- **"Designing for engagement" UX literature.** Same.
- **Most contemporary dating-app design literature.** The basic premise (1-on-1 matching from profile browsing) is rejected (`10` §6).
- **Pop-philosophy "rationalist" / EA literature** beyond specific narrow citations. Useful for some mechanism-design framing but generally insufficient on what makes a polis flourish (`02` §3).

## 8. The implicit standard

A reader who has read each work cited above will recognize themselves in nearly every architectural choice. A reader who has read *none* of them and disagrees on first encounter is invited to read a handful and reconsider. The platform's intellectual position is *deep* in the sense that it sits at the intersection of many longstanding traditions; it is not novel in any single component. Its novelty is in the *combination* and the *engineering*.
