# The Knowledge Growth Layer — Paideia

This document specifies how the platform makes citizens **smarter, wiser, and better-read** over the lifespan — not as a side effect but as a primary engineering goal. The Greek word is *paideia*: the lifelong cultivation of mind, character, and judgment that the polis owed every citizen.

Jiang's nine Great Books lectures (`TsD-8FGA84A`, `Ft2CuowGuYc`, `XRP407WsA0w`, `dkzr5A8IlLA`, `gXlcR7uHHdA`, `aS-NfPSPMu8`, `EBWTRvjZ1dw`, `YXTRlvFIRt8`, `6M1Z_V3WgOk`) are the spine of this layer. His thesis: every great book is one human being trying — with everything they had — to honestly answer a question that mattered to them. Reading them is the cheapest, most concentrated way to grow a soul.

## 1. The thesis

A citizen who reads ≥ 6 books a year, attends ≥ 8 debates a year, and discusses them with people who think differently *will become wiser* over a decade. This is not speculation — it is the consistent finding of every documented program from the Great Books seminars of St. John's College to the Catholic seminary curriculum to the Jewish chevruta tradition to the Russian intelligentsia of the 19th century.

The platform's job is to make this curriculum **the path of least resistance**, not a niche luxury for the leisured.

## 2. Tier-3 metrics this layer owns

From `01` §2 Tier-3:

- `books_completed_per_citizen_per_year` ≥ 6
- `weekly_debate_or_symposium_attendance_rate` ≥ 35%
- Indirectly: `cross-ideological-tie_rate` ≥ 20% (debate-formed ties tend to be cross-ideological)

These are not "engagement metrics." They are civilizational metrics. The platform exists in part to move them.

## 3. The Paideia Library — what is read

### 3.1 The Core Canon (curated, ~120 works, slowly rotating)

A sortitioned curatorial council (rotating 7 Elders + 3 working scholars across literature, philosophy, history, sciences, religion) maintains the Core Canon. Every citizen knows it; every Theater format may select from it; the Citizenship Reading (`04` §3.5) draws from a Newcomer subset.

Roughly partitioned (numbers approximate, not binding):

- **Greek / Roman foundations** (~20): Homer *Iliad* + *Odyssey*; Aeschylus *Oresteia*; Sophocles *Oedipus*, *Antigone*, *Philoctetes*; Euripides *Bacchae*, *Trojan Women*; Aristophanes *Clouds*; Thucydides; Plato *Republic*, *Symposium*, *Apology*; Aristotle *Nicomachean Ethics*, *Politics*, *Poetics*; Marcus Aurelius *Meditations*; Lucretius *On the Nature of Things*; Plutarch *Lives* (selected).
- **Religious / wisdom traditions** (~20): Hebrew Bible (Job, Ecclesiastes, Genesis 1-22); Gospel of Mark; Augustine *Confessions*; Aquinas (selections from *Summa*); Bhagavad Gita; Tao Te Ching; Analects of Confucius; Dhammapada; Quran (selections, with critical apparatus); Rumi; Eckhart; The Cloud of Unknowing; Julian of Norwich; Hasidic tales (Buber).
- **Medieval / early modern Western** (~15): Dante *Divine Comedy*; Boethius *Consolation*; Boccaccio *Decameron*; Cervantes *Quixote*; Shakespeare (*Hamlet*, *Lear*, *Tempest*, *Sonnets*); Pascal *Pensées*; Spinoza *Ethics*; Montaigne *Essays*.
- **Enlightenment to 19th century** (~20): Hume; Smith *Wealth of Nations* + *Theory of Moral Sentiments*; Kant *Critique of Pure Reason* (selections); Rousseau *Emile* + *Social Contract*; Federalist Papers; Tocqueville *Democracy in America*; Marx *Manifesto* + *Capital* (selections); Mill *On Liberty*; Darwin *Origin*; Nietzsche *Genealogy of Morals*; Tolstoy *War & Peace*, *Ivan Ilyich*; Dostoevsky *Karamazov*, *Notes from Underground*; Eliot *Middlemarch*; Melville *Moby-Dick*; Whitman *Leaves of Grass*.
- **20th century** (~20): Weber *Protestant Ethic*; Freud (selections); Joyce *Dubliners*; Woolf *To the Lighthouse*; Camus *Plague*; Solzhenitsyn *Gulag Archipelago* (vol 1); Arendt *Origins of Totalitarianism* + *Human Condition*; Orwell *1984* + essays; James Baldwin; Toni Morrison *Beloved*; Joan Didion; Wendell Berry; Heaney; Borges; García Márquez *One Hundred Years*; Calvino; Achebe; Kawabata; Lao She.
- **Sciences for non-scientists** (~10): Feynman *Six Easy Pieces*; Schrödinger *What Is Life?*; Watson *Double Helix*; Carson *Silent Spring*; Wilson *On Human Nature*; Diamond *Guns, Germs, Steel*; Kuhn *Structure of Scientific Revolutions*; Sacks; Kahneman *Thinking Fast and Slow*; Hofstadter *Gödel, Escher, Bach* (selections).
- **Civic & current** (~15): Putnam *Bowling Alone*; Oldenburg *Great Good Place*; Granovetter weak-ties paper; Vance *Hillbilly Elegy*; Saunders *A Swim in a Pond in the Rain*; Patrick Deneen *Why Liberalism Failed*; Rod Dreher *Benedict Option*; Wendell Berry *Unsettling of America*; Yuval Levin *A Time to Build*; Tristan Harris on attention; current-decade signal essays.

### 3.2 The Open Canon (citizen-proposed, ~unlimited)

Any 5 citizens (from 3+ different interest-clusters) can propose a Theater reading; if accepted by quorum, it joins the Open Canon, available to any Theater session. This is the long tail — modern novels, niche philosophical texts, scientific monographs, regional literatures.

### 3.3 The Local Canon (per-city, curated by city Elders)

Every polis maintains its own ~30-work shelf of texts about *this place* — local history, regional writers, neighborhood archives, civic documents. Reading the Local Canon is part of becoming-citizen-of-this-polis.

## 4. How citizens read together — Theater + Chevruta + Symposium

Three nested practices, in increasing depth:

### 4.1 Theater (one-shot, 10-25 citizens, 2 hours)

Per `06` §5. Read a book, watch a film, then talk about it. Lowest-friction entry point.

### 4.2 Chevruta (sustained paired reading, 2 citizens, weekly for 8-12 weeks)

Borrowed from the Jewish *chevruta* tradition: two citizens commit to reading one substantial work together — one chapter per week, alternating who leads the conversation, meeting in person (`12`). The matcher assembles chevruta pairs from citizens who've co-attended ≥ 1 Theater session and signaled chevruta interest. Pairs are matched for: (a) reading-pace compatibility, (b) M4 narrative-arc *complementarity* (not similarity — chevruta benefits from a thinking partner unlike you), (c) M13 schedule overlap.

Chevruta is high-cost (16 hours over 2-3 months); it produces some of the strongest ties the platform generates. Tier-1 metric #2 (strong-tie formation) is disproportionately delivered here.

### 4.3 Symposium-on-text (small dinner with shared reading, 5-7 citizens, 2.5 hours)

The Symposium format (`06` §2) with a pre-read leitmotif text — the conversation flows around the text rather than around an abstract prompt. Many citizens' first deep encounter with Dante / Aurelius / Dostoevsky is at a Symposium-on-text.

## 5. The Teaching Circle (Established+ citizens teach Newcomers)

Modeled on classical *paideia*: every Elder is expected, periodically, to teach. The platform invites Established citizens to convene Teaching Circles — small (5-10) groups of Newcomers reading a foundational text under the Elder's loose guidance.

The teacher is **not** a professional expert. They are a citizen who has read the text and is willing to read it again, in company. They are compensated only in service-credit (free meal at the venue, maybe a small honorarium per `13` — never enough to be a job). The role is conferred by sortition from the willing pool, not by application or self-selection.

This restores the Athenian conviction that *every citizen is a teacher of citizenship*, and the only intellectual aristocracy is the aristocracy of those who showed up to read.

## 6. The Lectures (occasional, one-many is allowed for *paideia* only)

A controlled exception to the one-many-broadcast prohibition: each polis hosts ~4 invited lectures per year on a serious topic by a serious scholar (visiting professor, working scientist, master craftsman). Audience: 50-300 citizens, free, in a public venue. The lecture is followed by a *symposiarchic* dinner where the speaker eats with 5-7 sortitioned citizens (rotation).

Why allowed: the *Symposium* (Plato's) explicitly includes one-person-speaking-at-length as a phase of the gathering; one citizen having prepared deeply on a topic is a gift to the rest. Why constrained: no celebrity-speaker culture, no honoraria above a modest scholarly stipend, no sponsorships, no recordings (Agora rule extended), no Q&A format that lets the audience signal-cluster.

## 7. The mistake-loop applied to learning

Per ContextGraph #406 / #523 mistake-loop pattern, applied to *paideia*:

- The platform tracks (with citizen consent) which texts each citizen has attested completing.
- The platform tracks (privately, per `08`) which Theater discussions produced strong post-event ratings vs. weak ones for each citizen.
- The matching algorithm learns, per citizen, which texts and which co-readers produce the deepest engagement, and biases future Theater invitations accordingly.
- No public "reading score" or "books-read leaderboard." The data feeds matching, not display.

## 8. Children & adolescents (Year-2+ scope)

The Greek polis educated its children together. Modern privatization of education (smartphones, screens, individual tutoring) is part of what destroyed *koinōnia*. Year-2 scope (after the platform proves on adults):

- Family-format Symposia where children attend alongside parents.
- Adolescent Theater circles where teens read alongside Elders (modeled on the Athenian *paidagōgos* tradition).
- Cross-generational chevruta pairings (Tier-3 metric #17 explicitly).

Out of Year-1 scope to keep the system shippable; not in conflict with the core design.

## 9. What is forbidden in this layer

- **No automated AI tutoring or AI book summaries** (Invariant I15 extended; the path to wisdom does not go through Cliffs Notes). The platform never publishes "AI summary of *Karamazov*" or "AI-generated discussion prompts." The friction of reading the actual book is the point.
- **No completion gamification** (no streaks, no badges, no "you've read 12 books this year!" trophies). The reward of reading *Brothers Karamazov* is having read *Brothers Karamazov*. The platform has nothing to add.
- **No "favorites" or public reading lists** (status leak T1).
- **No partnership / sponsorship by publishers, audiobook services, or book retailers** (Invariant I5). Citizens may use any library, any bookseller, any format (paper, audio, e-reader); the platform is agnostic.
